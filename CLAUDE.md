# Scentcast

Picks fragrances from David's own Fragrantica wardrobe for the weather, time of day and occasion. Static web app: vanilla ES modules, no build step, no dependencies. The same engine runs in Node for answers in the terminal.

## Answering "what should I wear…"

1. Get live picks from the engine: `node scripts/recommend.mjs --city "<city>" [--occasion <id>] [--slot day|night] --json`. Without an occasion or slot it plans today's next two windows together, in time order, like the app's Now (each slot's `pick`, then the full `ranked` list). For "this week" or a trip, add `--week` for a day + night plan over the next 7 days (with an occasion, only its slot). `--occasion` takes an id or label in any case ("date night"); the ids are in `src/occasions.js`. Ask David for his city if he hasn't given it. Every wardrobe bottle counts, Jake included; `--demo` applies `DEFAULT_HIDDEN` like the site. Bottles he switched off in the app (Wardrobe › Owned) are stored on his phone only, but his journal export carries them (`hidden`: the demo's, Jake among them unless he switched it back on): if he sends it, `--journal <export.json>` switches them off as the app does, rotates out what he wore recently, applies his own ratings and skin nudges (see Engine), tunes `--gaps`' use shares by the occasions his wears were logged for (as Insights does), and turns on the rotation nudge. Pass any others he mentions as `--exclude "Name,Name"` (part of a name is enough, whole words under 3 letters; it warns about misses and refuses a term matching more than 3 bottles). As in the app, picks leave switched-off bottles out, and suggestions and checks count them as owned (never suggested back; "You own it."). For a day further out ("Saturday's wedding", "a date on the 24th"), add `--date YYYY-MM-DD`: the forecast's windows when it reaches that far, typical weather for the date past it (the JSON says which). Every pick carries `spray` (see How much to spray).
2. For an occasion no preset covers (job interview, outdoor wedding, a flight), run the nearest preset(s), then adjust using `data/collection.json`: accord bar widths (30–100), note pyramid, season and day/night vote shares.
3. Reply with a short ranked shortlist drawn only from `data/collection.json`: each pick with its reason (weather, votes, accords), plus a runner-up.

For "what temperature suits X": `node scripts/recommend.mjs --ideal "<name>" [--json]` gives the feels-like range it wears best in (`comfortRange` in the engine, also on its bottle page) and where it makes the wardrobe's top 3 by day and by night, whatever the time of year (spans a degree or two apart joined). A name that matches several bottles takes the first and says so on stderr. `--ideal`, `--layer` and `--spray` answer for a switched-off bottle too, saying so on stderr (ranked among the rest for `--ideal`; never a partner for `--layer`).

For layering ("what goes with X", "cool layers"): `node scripts/recommend.mjs --layer "<name>" [--json]`, or `--layer all` for the best pairs in the wardrobe. No city needed; add `--city "<city>" [--slot day|night]` to score the pairs for that window's weather.

For styles ("what kind of scents do I own", "is X a skin scent"): `node scripts/recommend.mjs --mix [--json]` gives the wardrobe's style mix (how many bottles lean each way, which wear close) and each bottle's top two styles; `--styles "<name>" [--json]` gives one bottle's scores, from the wardrobe or, failing that, the Fragrantica dataset (see Scent styles). For "do I own two of the same", `--doubles [--json]` lists pairs that wear as one (see Insights and the Wishlist); David has none, and it names his closest pair.

For "what am I missing", "what should I buy" or "should I buy X": `--gaps` and `--check` (see Gap finder and buy check).

For buying ("more skin scents", "more like Liquid Brun", "something with iris and leather", "what smells like Aventus but cheaper"): `--style`, `--more-like`, `--notes` and `--dupes` (see Discover).

## Packing for a trip

For "what should I pack for London next week" or "3 days in Miami": `node scripts/recommend.mjs --pack --city "<city>" --from YYYY-MM-DD --to YYYY-MM-DD [--max N] [--occasion <id>] [--slot day|night] [--events "2026-10-20 night formal; 2026-10-21 date night"] [--json]`. It plans every day and night of the trip and packs the fewest bottles (up to `--max`, default 3) whose best for each window scores within `PLAN_MARGIN` of the whole wardrobe's top for it (`packTrip` in `src/pack.js`), at least two for a trip and three from a week, so there's something to trade off. It weighs every set of that size (a greedy cover missed two-bottle sets that covered a whole trip), and among sets that cover as much takes the one whose plan scores best, each pick less its rotation penalty: an extra bottle goes where it most improves the picks or spares a bottle worn day after day, not to a near copy of the first. On a trip of a week or more, three or more bottles that wear none on every day of the trip (by day or night) win when their plan scores within `VARIETY_MARGIN` (0.015) a window of the best and the most-worn bottle gets at least `VARIETY_RELIEF` (2) windows off (ten hot July days: Supremacy on 8 nights of 10 beside Essence de Blanc and Aristo, rather than every night), and with room under `--max` a fourth goes in when it rests one that much. Breaking a run once isn't variety (Cancún in January: Aristo on one of Supremacy's seven nights, at a cost), so that plan stands. When the set packed still wears one every day, `variety` names the bottle that would rest it (`slot`: what the bottle is worn for, day, night or null for both) and the text says so in those words ("Cream Velvet is worn every day of the trip; pack Liam too and each gets a day off.", "Supremacy Collector's Edition Pour Homme is worn every night of the trip; … a night off."): London in December, where any three bottles for twenty windows wear one every day, and the three that at least split the days cost 0.037 a window; `--max 4` packs Liam too. Two bottles for a week wear both every day, as before. Bottles David rates 2★ or lower only go in for a window nothing else suits. `--occasion` goes on every window of its slot (a work trip's days: `--occasion office`), `--slot` packs for days or nights only, and an event overrides both on its window: a date, an occasion (id or label) and an optional day or night before or after it ("night out" and "date night" are occasions; "night formal" and "formal night" a slot and one). The reply's `text` reads like "Pack Cream Velvet (every day), CH Men Prive (5 nights) and Sticky Fingers (4 nights and formal wear on Dec 15)"; each bottle's `why` leads with the windows it's worn and says where else it would do; `plan` says which to wear when (packed bottles that suit a window take turns by the rotation penalty), and `uncovered` lists windows nothing packed suits, with the bottle that would ("…; Terra would be, if there's room"). Lead with the text, then the plan.

Weather comes from `tripWindows` in `src/weather.js`: the forecast for `FORECAST_DAYS` (10 days with today), where at least two of the blend's models have a value, then typical weather (`typicalWindow`: the same dates, give or take three days, in each of the last three years Open-Meteo's archive has whole, averaged). Open-Meteo forecasts 16 days, but ICON stops after about 7 and GEM after 10; on `score-forecasts.py`'s airports typical weather missed window temperatures by 5.5°F, the blend by 3.6 a week out, GFS and ECMWF alone by 4.0 and growing half a degree a day, GFS alone by 5.0 (Open-Meteo keeps past runs only seven days out, so ten is that growth's reach). A typical window's `pop` is the share of those past dates with 1 mm of rain or snow in it (the archive codes a trace as drizzle), and its condition is a wet one only when most were. Typical windows carry `typical: true`; say so when you answer ("typical London weather for mid-December"). `picksFor` in `src/pack.js` ranks one date and slot the same way (`--date`), and `tripPlan` packs from a place and dates. Trips run up to 31 days and a year ahead.

The app has the same planner as Wear › Trip (`src/views/wear/trip.js`): a place from its own city search (it never moves David's location), dates, events (a day, day or night, and an occasion; one a window) and two to four bottles, three by default as `packTrip`. It fetches with `tripWindows` and packs with `packTrip` itself, so changing only the events or the count packs again from the saved weather, offline. It shows the packed bottles on a shelf, each with the windows it's worn (`roleOf`: "Every day", "3 nights, formal wear on Fri and a date on Sun") and its commonest lead reason, the windows nothing packed suits (and, after a week, a bottle worn every day; `variety`) with a button for one more bottle, then a plan a row a day where ≈ marks typical weather and a window nothing packed suits says "Not ideal", and a packing list to copy or share (`packingText`: "Sun, Oct 25: Terra by day; date night: Sticky Fingers"). The last trip stays on the device (`scentcast.trip`: its inputs and the windows fetched; `loadTrip`, `saveTrip`), and weather more than six hours old is fetched again when it opens. A trip under way can still change: it keeps its start (`checkTrip` refuses a past start only when it was moved) and the weather saved for the days gone by, since the forecast has none for them (`windowsFor` fetches from today on), and new events go on the days still to come; "typical weather from" counts from today. Plan adds an event filled in but not yet added, and the form is inert while the weather loads, so what's saved is what was asked. Packing a big list (more than 1,000 bottle-windows, a fifth of a second for 120 bottles over a month) runs after the page paints, the last plan dimmed until it's in. Its bottle pages read contexts `trip:<date>:<slot>`.

The Occasion pane's "When and where?" (`src/views/wear/when.js`) ranks an occasion for another date (up to a year out) or city, and its heading says so: "Formal · Sat night in Chicago". Today here reads the app's forecast, a later date here the week's while it reaches, and anything else fetches that date's two windows once a session (`tripWindows`, so typical weather past `FORECAST_DAYS`, marked "Typical weather"). Its toggle is always Day | Night, and on today the date pill names the shown window's date (Tomorrow once today's daytime is over). Only today here takes the rotation nudge (`rankContext(…, { nudge })`): the journal can't say what will be idle on a later date, and a wedding wants the best bottle; a nudged pick there is "Due a wear", not "Best for". It's for the session; the next visit starts from today, here.

## How much to spray

`node scripts/recommend.mjs --spray "<name>" [--city "<city>" [--date YYYY-MM-DD] [--slot day|night]] [--occasion <id>] [--json]` gives a count and where for the next windows at a place, or for a hot sticky day, a mild day and a cold night without one. `sprayGuide(record, conditions, occasion, skin)` in `src/spray.js` starts from 3 and moves by sillage (1.5 sprays a level, `performanceLevel`, so skin nudges count) and longevity, the strength the name claims (Extrait, Parfum, Elixir, Intense, EDT, Cologne…; half as much once 50 voters rated its performance, since concentration doesn't decide sillage; a body mist by the dataset's `mist` flag too, since most don't say so in their name), heat and sticky air (fewer, low on the chest under a shirt unless the bottle is fresh and quiet; scaled by `heaviness`, so a gourmand loses nearly two sprays and a fresh citrus a quarter of one, since rich notes turn cloying and fresh ones burn off), cold (more, and on a collar or scarf, which holds it) and the occasion (Office at most 2, a date close and at most 4, a night out more). It returns `sprays: [lo, hi]` (1–6, one number when it lands near one), `where`, a one- or two-sentence `text` with no temperature (its second leads with what moved the count the way it went, and a big push the other way follows with "though": "Sticky heat makes rich notes cloying, though it stays close to the skin"), `why` and an optional `note`, the most useful one: a loud bottle at 1–2 sprays (you'll stop smelling it long before others do), one that fades early by the crowd's votes or David's skin, or a fresh one in heat (take it along to top up), or a close-wearing one (this many won't crowd anyone; never when it projects more on him). A perfume oil (named so, as Discover's cards read it) gets dabs instead (`unit: 'dab'`: a dab or two on the wrists and neck). `test/spray.test.js` pins a table of cases collectors would agree with (a beast-mode extrait on a hot office day is 1; a light cologne on a cold night out 4–6; CH Men Prive on a hot sticky day 1–2, Essence de Blanc 2–3); add a case before retuning.

## Collection data

`data/collection.jsonl` is the raw scrape, one line per bottle. `data/collection.json` is generated from it by `npm run build:data`, so edit the jsonl.

To add or refresh a bottle:
1. Get its line. A bottle in the Fragrantica dataset (below; `python3 scripts/decant.py similar <id or "name brand">` finds it) needs no browser: `python3 scripts/decant.py add collection <id>` appends or replaces it. A bottle newer than the dataset needs the extractor: open its page with Claude in Chrome (David is logged in as @doeszen), run `scripts/fragrantica-extract.js` on the rendered page with the javascript tool (it returns one JSON line, clicking "Show votes" for the note votes, `noteVotes`, and back off), and append or replace that line in `data/collection.jsonl`.
2. `python3 scripts/decant.py extras`, `npm run build:data`, `npm run fit` and `npm test`.
3. If it's a dupe, add its original (below). Add its Parfumo line, and its original's (below).

`python3 scripts/decant.py refresh` takes newer vote counts from a new dataset version into every `data/*.jsonl` line, and rewrites `data/extras.jsonl` (below).

Dupes and their originals: `data/dupes.json` pairs a dupe's slug with its original's, and `data/originals.jsonl` holds the originals' lines (`decant.py add originals <id>`). The build gives each dupe a summary of its original's votes as `original`; the engine leans on it for a dupe's season and day/night split, the bottle page says "Inspired by", and `npm run fit` counts the originals as voted bottles. Find the original in the dataset's "smells like" votes: `python3 scripts/decant.py similar <id>` lists them, and the top bottle from another brand released no later is the curated original for 18 of the 19 dupes. `decant.py dupes` checks every pair against the votes and names unpaired bottles whose votes point to an original. When a dupe's list holds only fellow clones (Essence de Blanc's), run `similar` on one of them, or read the reviews: the raw HTML of a perfume page has its 200 newest `[itemprop="reviewBody"]`, and the most-named perfume after "dupe of", "clone of", "smells like" and the like is nearly always it. Ask David when the votes are split.

Longevity, sillage and layering votes come from Parfumo (Fragrantica's layout has none). `python3 scripts/parfumo.py` fetches them without a browser into `data/parfumo.jsonl`, one line per bottle by Fragrantica id: `path`, Parfumo id `pid`, `[average of 10, votes]` for scent, longevity and sillage, `layer`, the page's "Good to layer" list (`[Parfumo id, community consensus %]`, up to 30, best first), and `occasion`, its Occasion chart (% Daily, Leisure, Business, Night Out, Evening, Sport) with `occasionVotes`. `--search "<name brand>"` lists candidate paths, `--add <fragrantica id> <path>` adds a line, and no arguments refreshes every line; then `npm run build:data`. Requests go 2 seconds apart, and the script saves after each bottle: Parfumo's Cloudflare challenges a run partway (HTTP 403 on every page, for an hour or more), and it prints the command that resumes. A browser tab that has passed the challenge still fetches: in October 2026 the occasion charts came from the same requests run in a parfumo.com tab's console. Brand slugs vary (`Afnan_Perfumes`, `lattafa-pride`, `Goldfield__Banks`), names can differ (Atlantis Extrait is "Atlantis"), and Parfumo splits concentrations (Gris Charnel EDP and extrait), so check the match. The build joins the ratings as `parfumo` and the lists as `layeredWith` (see Engine). Originals get lines too: dupes have few layering votes of their own, and their originals hold most of the wardrobe's.

Bottles a first-time visitor never sees (demo-safe) are `DEFAULT_HIDDEN` in `src/hidden.js`. They only tidy the demo: a shared link or someone's own list shows them (`hiddenFor`). A device stores its own hides (`scentcast.hidden`, applied in every list) apart from the defaults it switched back on in the demo (`scentcast.shown`), so hiding a bottle in a shared link never drags a default along (`setHidden`).

`data/calibration.jsonl` (built to `data/calibration.json`) holds 52 well-voted bottles from across the accord families (Tobacco Vanille, Acqua di Gio, Angel, Terre d'Hermès…). Only `scripts/fit-accords.mjs` reads them, to fit accord heaviness on more than the wardrobe's own votes; the app never loads them. Add bottles with `decant.py add calibration <id>`, aiming for accords with few bottles behind them (the fit prints the count per accord).

The full owned list is the "Perfumes I Have" shelf at `https://www.fragrantica.com/@doeszen#wardrobe`. The profile home mixes in his Want list.

Fragrantica gotchas:
- The "When To Wear" and "Rating" cards render client-side from obfuscated embedded data. Read the rendered DOM (the extractor waits for it); raw HTML fetches lack those numbers.
- The current page layout has no longevity or sillage votes; those come from Parfumo (above), or the dataset's where Parfumo lacks a bottle.
- The javascript tool's output is cut at about 1,000 characters. For many lines, collect them in the page (localStorage), write them into a `<pre>` as the page body and read it with `get_page_text`. A script stored in localStorage runs on each page with `await new Function('return (async () => {' + code + '})()')()`. A javascript call over 45 seconds times out but keeps running in the page.
- Tool output that includes raw page HTML can trip a cookie/query-string filter; return parsed fields only.
- Fragrantica sometimes shows a Cloudflare "Verify you are human" check; ask David to click it.
- To find a bottle's page, open `https://www.fragrantica.com/search/?query=<name brand>` and read the `/perfume/` links once results render (the sidebar also lists David's own bottles).
- Bottle images hotlink `fimgs.net/mdimg/perfume-thumbs/dark-m.<id>.2x.webp` (transparent background).
- A brand page (`/designers/<Brand>.html`) lists its perfume links in the raw HTML, so `fetch` from a Fragrantica tab finds page URLs without the slow search. Fetch one at a time: a burst of about 40 got HTTP 429.
- Chrome throttles timers in a background tab, so client-rendered search results crawl there; perfume pages still render their cards.
- Newer pages use accords like cherry, tuberose, yellow floral, marine, salty, aldehydic, coffee, rum, smoky and patchouli. Each needs a row in `src/accords.js` and a `FAMILY_ALIAS` in `src/layering.js` (the family it layers like); `test/accords.test.js` names any that are missing.

## Fragrantica dataset

`data/fragrantica/` holds every Fragrantica perfume with accords (137,437), from Le Decanteur's Kaggle dataset (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0: the app's footer always credits it, since even the demo's records carry values derived from it, and `data/fragrantica/README.md` carries the license). The app searches it after the demo and catalog, and share links resolve its ids, which are Fragrantica's like the demo's. `src/fragrantica.js` turns a row into an engine record (`source: 'fragrantica'`), loading one search-index file per query and one record file per added bottle.

`scripts/decant.py` builds it (file layout and keys in its docstring). For a new dataset version: `python3 scripts/decant.py download` (needs David's Kaggle token in `~/.kaggle/access_token`), `build`, then `python3 scripts/decant.py extras`, `node scripts/fit-profiles.mjs --write`, `node scripts/fit-occasions.mjs --write`, `node scripts/fit-pool-styles.mjs --write`, `npm run build:data` and `npm test`. The build rewrites most of its 1,800 files, about 32 MB gzipped. `decant.py pool`, `about` and `extras` remake one piece each (every command reads the whole 555 MB dump, about 40 seconds); none of them touch `r/` or `i/`.

Beyond the search and records, the dataset has what a bottle's page shows and signals derived from its votes:
- `pool.json` and `pool-notes.json`: the suggestion pool for gap filling and Discover, 9,644 bottles: 500+ rating votes, plus rising ones (100–499 votes, recent popularity 1,000+: the new Middle-Eastern releases David buys). Columnar, one array per key, rows by brand then name (sort by `rv` for most voted). It holds everything the engine scores on, exact but for rating, Fragrantica longevity and sillage and the like-shares (2 decimals) and the longevity and sillage vote counts (2 significant digits; `rank()` scores stay within 0.005 of the `r/` record's), and the signals below. `pool-notes.json` holds each bottle's 8 strongest notes, with votes in tenths of the top note's. 1.8 + 0.4 MB raw, 573 + 151 KB gzip -9 (Node's), over the 600 KB aimed for; `test/pool.test.js` holds them under 730 KB together. To trim: 6 notes instead of 8 (about −28 KB), season and time votes to 2 significant figures (−25 KB) or bar widths to even numbers (−10 KB), the last two at some cost to scoring exactly like `r/`. `loadPool()` in `src/fragrantica.js` fetches both once, next to the module, so a module Worker under `src/` can call it (load it when a feature needs it, never on boot) and `fromPool(pool, i)` makes bottle i's engine record (`source: 'fragrantica'`) with the signals as `extras`.
- `x/<id % 512>.json`: the about data of the 48,291 bottles with 30+ rating votes, 13.8 MB raw, 6.0 MB gzipped. `loadAbout(id)` reads one with readable keys: `sentiment` (the rating histogram), `value` {avg, votes}, `owners` {have, had, want}, `crowdGender` {lean, votes}, `popularity` {all, recent}, `perfumers`, `line` (Fragrantica's collection), `pros` (top 3) and `cons` (top 2) as {text, up, down}, `smellsLike` (top 5 {id, up, down}), `alsoLiked` (10 ids: co-preference, "fans also like", not smells-like), `flags`.
- `data/extras.jsonl` (`decant.py extras`): the same for every bottle in the wardrobe, catalog and originals that the dataset has (one newer than it has neither `about` nor `extras`, so treat both as optional), plus their signals and `clones` (up to 6 bottles from other brands, released no earlier, valued 3.8+, that voters say smell like it: 20+ net votes, 2:1, either way round). The build joins it like `parfumo.jsonl`: the signals as `extras`, the rest as `about` (and its histogram as `sentiment` where a line has none, as `decant.py add` lines don't). A dupe's `original` gets the original's `extras`, `parfumo` and `performance`, and `siblings`, its other clones (Cream Velvet's Bianco Latte: Lattafa Eclaire and four more), since the app never loads `originals.json`.

The signals, on pool and wardrobe records alike (`extras`):
- `value`: value for money, 1 overpriced to 5 great value, few votes leaning to 3.1. It matches the AI summaries' "affordable" and "overpriced" at AUC 0.98 and barely tracks the rating. The pool's median is 3.0 ("Great value" from 3.8, where "Prefer great value" draws its line, "Good value" from 3.4, "Fair price" from 2.4, else "Pricey": every screen says it with `valueWord` in `src/ui.js`, and risk and ratings with `riskWord` and `ratingMark`); the wardrobe's is 4.2, so value is a taste for David, not just a badge.
- `safe`: blind-buy safety, the pool percentile (0–100) of the like + love share less 4 × the hate share. The hate share predicts an AI "not a safe blind buy" far better than the rating does.
- `keep`: the share of owners who keep it, over what pages that old keep (+0.1 or more: people keep it).
- `lean`: the crowd's gender vote, −1 feminine to +1 masculine. A soft taste signal, never a filter: David owns crowd-feminine bottles.
- `now`: the pool percentile of recent popularity (about two years).
- `owners`: how many Fragrantica members own it ("have"; the pool keeps it as 4 × log2(1 + owners), so about 2 significant digits). `reach`: the share of the bottles its fans also like that are among the pool's 5% most owned, at most its brand's median + 0.15 (see Candidates and similarity), null when none of them is in the pool and its brand has under 5 pool bottles.
- `like`: up to 3 bottles voters say it smells like (5+ net votes, 2:1). `family`: the ones among its top 6 voters call one scent (30+ agreeing votes, 2.5 × the disagreeing: dupes, clones, twins), for telling a near-copy of an owned bottle from a new direction. Links run one way; an owned bottle's own `about.smellsLike` (with votes) and `extras.family` cover the other. `alt`: up to 3 cheaper alternatives, for bottles valued 2.6 or less: smells-like either way round (10+ net votes, 2:1), valued 1+ higher (Aventus → Club de Nuit Intense Man; it finds 12 of the wardrobe's 19 dupe pairs on its own).
- `flags`: `mist` (a body mist or spray, see Candidates and similarity); `discontinued`, `reformulated`, `notBlindBuy` and `hardToFind` from AI summary cons more voters agree with than not. `discontinued` counts only a con said of the bottle itself: pros praise "an alternative to the discontinued X", and cons like "weaker than the discontinued EDT", "may be discontinued" or "discontinued in some regions" don't count (`says_discontinued` in `decant.py`); "extremely difficult to find in stock" is `hardToFind`. `brigaded`: more than 1.5 ratings per owner with a lopsided spread (30%+ hate or 85%+ love), or more than 5 whatever the spread: SpongeBob, Bolsonaro, the Pride editions, Sauvage Eau Forte (leave these out of suggestions). Louis Vuitton bottles people rate from a tester run 1.5 to 2 ratings per owner with an ordinary spread, so the ratio alone isn't enough (Pur Ambre, loved by 87%, still gets flagged). `polarizing`, a hate share in the pool's top tenth.

The pros and cons are Fragrantica's AI summary of its members' reviews. Quote them as "Fragrantica members' summary", a few per bottle, with the dataset credit.

Gotchas:
- Records keep accords as Fragrantica's bar widths, which our extractor reads and the engine's weights are tuned on: 30 + 0.7 × the dataset's strength (`barWidth` and `barStrength` in `src/accords.js`).
- Note weights are 100 × (votes / the top note's votes)^1.61; records turn them back into relative `noteVotes`.
- Its season and day/night votes are exact and match our scrapes; its ratings can come from an older index.
- Some descriptions hold a raw U+2028, which Node's readline and Python's `splitlines()` treat as a line break; read the raw dump line by line in Python, as `decant.py` does.
- Some brands are in capitals (MAISON ASRAR, PARIS CORNER, ALREHAB PERFUMES). Records read them in title case where they're made (`brandCase` in `src/fragrantica.js`, `brand_case` in `scripts/build_collection.py`; past 4 letters, so BDK and YSL stay); the dataset's files and page slugs keep Fragrantica's.

## Other people's collections

The app ranks one of three lists: `demo` (David's wardrobe, `data/collection.json`), `mine` (bottles a visitor adds; full records stored in their localStorage) or a shared link (`?c=id,id&n=Name`, opened as a temporary view). Logic lives in `src/collections.js` and `src/app/state.js`.

Search covers the demo, the catalog, then the dataset (offline, the first two still answer). Discover marks a result already in the list being ranked or in Mine; "I own it" adds the bottle to Mine and ranks from Mine, and when that switches lists (from David's wardrobe or a shared link) its toast says so and offers the way back. Pasted lists only tick a hit whose name words were all typed (`nameMatches`); the rest come unticked with an "Add it yourself" option, and one already in Mine comes marked "In Mine", not ticked (the button and toast count only what goes in). Bottles no source has are entered by hand ("Add it yourself", `src/custom.js`): up to 6 accords, strongest first, plus optional seasons and day/night, blended with what the accords predict (Engine). Their ids are `my:<random>`, and share links carry them whole as `my:<id>~<base64url>`.

When someone sends their Fragrantica profile (they follow the guide at https://claude.ai/code/artifact/25b353d3-857f-4341-89b3-230131f8c484):
1. Open `https://www.fragrantica.com/@<name>#wardrobe` with Claude in Chrome and run `scripts/fragrantica-wardrobe.js`. It lists the pages on their "Perfumes I Have" shelf; `found` below `expected` means the shelf didn't fully load.
2. ## Engine

`src/engine.js` is pure: fragrances + conditions in, ranked tiers with reasons out. A bottle's score is `scoreOf(parts, occasion)`: the weighted parts (`DAILY_WEIGHTS` or `OCCASION_WEIGHTS`) less its rotation penalty; anything that scores bottles outside `rank()` (the gap finder) calls it rather than copying the weights. Tiers are by rank (3 S, 5 A, 6 B, rest C), and a tier also needs its floor score (`TIER_FLOORS`: S 0.75, A 0.6), so in a small collection on a sweltering day a bottle that's "too rich for sticky heat" isn't S just for being third. `test/engine.test.js` pins behaviour on the real collection (hot humid day puts fresh scents on top, cold night puts heavy ones on top, occasion sanity), so run `npm test` after any tuning. Accord heaviness and chip colors live in `src/accords.js`: each accord has a hand-set `prior` and a `weight` that `npm run fit` (`scripts/fit-accords.mjs`) pulls toward the season votes of every bottle in `data/`. Rerun it after adding bottles; `test/accords.test.js` fails until you do, and also when the data has an accord `src/accords.js` lacks. A new accord gets `weight` equal to its `prior`. Occasion profiles live in `src/occasions.js`; every accord over 1.5% of the strength in `data/` needs an entry in each (0 marks it considered and neutral), and `test/occasions.test.js` names any that lack one.

The occasion part reads on one fixed scale for every collection: 0 at the dataset's 2nd percentile of `occasionRaw` for that occasion, 1 at its 98th (`OCCASION_SCALE` in `src/occasions.js`, over the 8,201 bottles with 500+ rating votes), never narrower than the accords' fit alone spans (`accordFit`, the raw fit without performance or votes). Most dataset bottles fade by Fragrantica's votes (their median longevity level is -0.5 against the wardrobe's middle), so Office's wish to last pulled its 98th percentile from 0.533 to 0.362, under most lasting fresh bottles, and eight of David's read above 0.9 (Sauvage among them, in his Office top 3 on mild nights); its top is now the accords' 98th (0.446), and six read above 0.9, one above 0.99. Date Night, Night Out and Formal keep their own (Formal's moves 0.003). Within `OCCASION_KNEE` of either end it bends toward 0 or 1 instead of stopping, so bottles past the dataset's range keep their order: Parfumo's occasion votes, which dataset bottles lack, carry a few owned bottles past the 98th percentile (a hard cap tied five demo bottles as perfect date-night fits). Anything comparing owned bottles with dataset ones on this scale (the gap finder) should leave out `parfumo.occasion` on the owned side, so both stand on the same evidence. Stretching each list's own min..max made any wardrobe's best bottle a perfect fit, read two bottles as 0 and 1, and let a narrow spread drown out the weather (a summer citrus topped Outdoors at 30°F). After changing a profile, a performance wish or the dataset, refit it with `node scripts/fit-occasions.mjs --write`; `test/occasions.test.js` fails until you do.

Reason text never names a raw accord label or a temperature: `ACCORD_NOUN`, `COOLING` and `WARMING` turn accords into phrases ("Warm spice for the cold", "Bright citrus cuts the heat", "Woods & aromatics suit casual wear"), only fresh accords cut the heat, and the rain reason names the bottle's own strongest rain accord. Reasons about the bottle itself (its accords against the weather or occasion, performance, recent wears) sort before the season and time-of-day votes. `test/reasons.test.js` scans every reason over the wardrobe, the catalog and two dataset shards.

Recent wears push a bottle down for ten days, fading (`ROTATION_PENALTY`, 0.2 today to 0.01 nine days ago; "Worn N days ago"). Top scores sit within a few hundredths, so the old three-day memory cycled three bottles through a month of steady weather; ten days rotates 9 to 17.

The rotation nudge pulls neglected bottles in (`nudge` on `rank` and `planWindows`, on in the app's Now, Week and Occasion for today here, and in the CLI whenever there's a journal): a bottle unworn for `NUDGE_AFTER` (30) days or more that scores within `PLAN_MARGIN` of the top and shares the top pick's tier moves to the front ("Not worn in 41 days"), so it never trades an S pick for an A; when the top pick is a neglected bottle already, nothing moves and nothing says so. Days count from the last wear or plan before the window; a bottle never worn counts from the journal's first logged wear, or from `added` when a record carries the date it joined, so nothing is neglected before the journal is a month old, and it says "Not worn yet" (the journal's age isn't the bottle's). Bottles David rates 2★ or lower (`DISLIKED`) sit out on purpose and are never nudged. A plan nudges at most once every three days, and a window with a logged wear keeps it. `test/nudge.test.js` runs it on synthetic histories.

Your own ratings ride on records as `personal` (`src/skin.js`: per bottle on the device, `scentcast.skin`, stars 1–5 and "on my skin" longevity and sillage -1/0/1; `applySkin(records, skin)` attaches them, and the journal export carries them). Stars count twice. `quality()` takes 80% from them (1★ reads 0, 3★ neutral, 5★ 1) and 20% from the crowd's, but quality is only 5% of a score, so `rank` adds `parts.personal` too (`personalPart`, `PERSONAL_PICK`: -0.1 at 1★, -0.06 at 2★, -0.01 at 3★, +0.02 at 5★; with quality's own share about -0.13, -0.08, -0.02 and +0.03): a 1★ or 2★ bottle drops out of the top 3 unless it's clearly the only fit (it led the 4th by about 0.13 or 0.08; in David's wardrobe the lead is under 0.05 half the time), a 3★ one gives way to a close second, a 5★ one wins near ties (half the time it sits second to fourth) without taking every window, and 4★ (what most kept bottles would get) moves a bottle only through quality. `test/skin.test.js` checks both ends over 512 windows on David's wardrobe and a third of it. The gap finder scores owned bottles with `parts.personal` too (`fitTable`, as `rank()` does), and pool records carry no stars, so its reference stays on the crowd's footing; a bottle he rates 2★ or lower covers nothing there (`gapContext`'s `worn` leaves it out of coverage, so it's never "your best"), though it still counts as owned (its twins are Redundant) and toward his styles. In a plan (`planWindows`), such a bottle never takes a slot for variety: it's planned only where it ranks first with no bottle new to the plan within `PLAN_MARGIN`. The app ranks `rankedFragrances()` (`src/app/state.js`): the switched-on bottles with `applySkin(…, loadSkin())`, cached until the list, its switches or the stored ratings change (`skinStamp` in `src/skin.js`, so whatever saves a rating only has to render again); trips, events and the pool's questions (`poolInputs()`) take the same records, so a bottle he rates 2★ or lower covers nothing on Insights either. Reasons say "You rate it 5★" (4★ and up) or "You rate it 2★" (2★ and down) in place of "Crowd favorite". Each skin nudge moves `performanceLevel` by half a level (`PERSONAL_SHIFT`), which reaches the occasions that care about performance and spray counts, not daily picks; where an occasion cares, the reason is yours ("Lasts longer on you", "Sits closer on you") in place of the crowd's. Scent styles still read voters' sillage.

Sticky heat is judged by dew point (`dewF` on forecast windows, from temperature and humidity), not relative humidity: 88°F at 55% is oppressive, 72°F at 85% is not.

The forecast's temperature, feels-like and humidity are the mean of four global models (`BLEND_MODELS` in `src/weather.js`: GFS, ECMWF, ICON, GEM), averaged in `blendModels` as it's fetched; current conditions, weather codes and rain chances stay Open-Meteo's default. Open-Meteo's default alone (GFS in the US) ran warm and dry against airport readings. `python3 scripts/score-forecasts.py [START END]` rescores the models and blends on 14 US airports (past forecasts from Open-Meteo's Previous Runs API, readings from the Iowa Mesonet ASOS archive); rerun it before changing the blend.

Which season the weather "is" comes from `SEASON_CLIMATE`: the mean and spread of each season's day and night feels-like over three years in ten temperate cities, fitted by `node scripts/fit-seasons.mjs --write` from Open-Meteo's archive (cached in `.cache/climate/`). Spring and fall weather overlap, so the calendar splits that share (`TRANSITION_SPLIT`), fading to an even split as it gets colder (`TRANSITION_COLD`, full at 58°F, even at 38°F): spring votes go to florals and greens, which a cold April night doesn't call for any more than a cold October one. Midwinter and midsummer days lean toward their calendar season by up to `CALENDAR_BOOST`, easing in and out smoothly rather than stepping at month boundaries, and the calendar fades out toward the equator (`TROPICS`, 25° to 15° of latitude). Without a date, `targetSeasonWeights` reads no calendar at all; `comfortRange` and `--ideal` use that, so a bottle's range is the same all year (Amber Empire's swung from 30°F in April to 56°F in October).

Season and day/night shares are read through `steadyShares`: a split from few votes leans toward a prediction, so a 100-vote indie bottle can't swing on a handful of votes. The prediction (`predictedShares` in `src/custom.js`) is the bottle's accords' profiles (`src/profiles.js`: the season and night shares each accord pulls toward, fitted by `scripts/fit-profiles.mjs` on the dataset's 12,000 well-voted bottles), averaged with `like`, the votes of the bottles Fragrantica voters say it smells like (from the dataset, on every record that has them). It counts as `PRIOR_VOTES`, measured on bottles held out of the fit: 36 season and 16 day/night votes from the accords alone, 60 and 26 with `like`. A dupe's original counts as another 120 votes for both: across the 18 dupes it predicts the dupe's split far better than the accords do (refit by comparing them again if the list grows). Votes that say nothing count as none (`flatVotes`, `votedShares`): the least-voted season drawn by half the raters or more within 3 points of the rest, or one exact count on 3 seasons or on 4 of the 6 season and day/night boxes (Elyon Dubai's 2025 releases, some Louis Vuitton pages and Shalimar Millésime Tonka's 432 on winter, fall, day and night: 22 pool bottles, none owned), which read as votes made perfect all-rounders of rose-ouds (Elyon Infinity topped 114 of 512 test windows added to David's wardrobe, 17 now). Day and night votes count as flat only on a page whose seasons are too: half the raters ticking both is a bottle worn either way (Sauvage EDP, Bleu de Chanel Parfum), and a scraped page's "2.4k" counts tie by rounding (Liam, Vintage Radio). Reasons say "A winter scent", not "Voted…", and "A daytime scent", not "Daytime favorite", for them and for any split nobody voted on. The gap finder and Discover's season and day/night filters read the same steadied shares. The bottle page still shows the raw votes. Hand-entered and never-voted bottles carry the prediction as their split.

Occasions can also want a loud or quiet, lasting or fleeting bottle (`performance` in `src/occasions.js`: Office moderate and lasting the working day, Night Out loud, Formal and Date lasting). A wish for less counts the level only down to `LESS_ENOUGH` (-0.3) and past it back the other way at half the rate (`performanceFit`): counted all the way down, Office's quiet wish made Bath & Body Works' Book Loft, gone by lunch, the pool's best office bottle, Jake David's on hot days, and Dark Door Intense beat Dior Homme Intense, its original. With Office's longevity wish at 0.5 (was 0.3) the pool's office leaders on a cool day are Armani Code Parfum, Gentleman EDT Intense, Rehab and Lalique Pour Homme, Book Loft sits 205th, Dior Homme Intense 2011 (154th) beats Dark Door Intense (193rd), and David's long-lasting fresh clones make Office one of his strengths; nothing of his a fifth of a level louder than the middle (Sauvage and up) makes his Office top 3, day or night (`test/engine.test.js`). Reasons still read the plain level ("Stays close to the skin" stays true of a whisper). `performanceLevel` reads Parfumo's longevity and sillage as -1..1 around the data's medians (7.8 and 7.4), shrunk toward them when few voted; without Parfumo ratings it reads the dataset's Fragrantica votes (`performance`, put on Parfumo's scale by a line fitted on the 53 bottles both rate), and a bottle with neither sits in the middle. Daily picks don't use them. Parfumo voters' own occasion shares nudge Office, Date Night, Night Out and Outdoors (`VOTED_OCCASIONS`: Business, Evening, Night Out, Sport), where they agree with the accord profiles (r 0.54 to 0.74 on the 53 bottles that have them); Casual, Formal and Cozy have no clean match and use the accords alone.

Season and time fit are half "within its comfort zone" and half "its specialty" (lift over an even vote split). The specialty half keeps flat all-rounders from winning every mild day.

Layering (`src/layering.js`, the "Layer it with" section of a bottle's page) is separate from ranking. Notes map to accord families by name (`NOTE_FAMILIES`, first match wins, so specific names go first; a note between families, like labdanum or ambroxan, lists several, main one first, the rest at half weight), and each bottle's profile blends those with its accords. Notes count more in the heart and base than on top, and by their votes over the bottle's average (`noteVotes`, between `NOTE_VOTE_FLOOR` and `NOTE_VOTE_CAP`); a shared note voters barely smell doesn't count as a bridge. A pair scores on complement (`PAIRS`, symmetric, negatives for clashes), a bridge of shared families or notes (near-duplicates are marked down), and heaviness contrast; the heavier bottle is sprayed first. Each partner's average pairing across the rest of the collection (without the bottle being paired, so the CLI and the page agree) is subtracted, so all-rounders don't top every list. Those averages need every pair's score, so `pairingMeans` keeps them for the last collection asked about (with the open bottle joined), a bottle counting as kept while its id, accords and notes are (a star rating's new record still is): each open reads its averages off the kept pairs in O(n) rather than redoing the n² pass (at 150 bottles in Node, about 220 ms an open before, 3 ms after the first); `layerPicks` hands a page drawn twice its last answer. Over 12 bottles, the page doesn't run the pass inside the tap: it draws the section as a skeleton and `warmPairing` works the pass out in slices at idle (`pairingKept` says when it's in), then the section draws again. Given a weather window (the page always has one), `blendFit` reads the pair like the engine reads one bottle (both bottles' season votes, and their average heaviness against the weather's target) and a pair below `BLEND_OK` loses points; without one, two heavy bottles take a flat penalty instead. `test/layering.test.js` fails when a note in the data maps to no family (add it to `NOTE_FAMILIES`), or when `PAIRS` has no rule for more than a fifth of how the data's families meet (add rules for the gaps it names; a 0 marks a pair as considered and neutral). Rescale `COMPLEMENT_FLOOR` and `COMPLEMENT_SPAN` to the complement's 10th–90th percentile after big table changes.

Parfumo's layering votes feed the same ranking. The build turns each `layer` list into `layeredWith` on wardrobe and catalog bottles (`[{id, consensus, via}]`, partners within those two files, the same both ways), with a dupe standing in for its original on either side: Layton + Althaïr votes pair Détour Noir with Liquid Brun, and `via` names the two bottles voted on. A voted pair gains `COMMUNITY_WEIGHT` × consensus, given a weather window scaled from nothing at a blend fit of `BLEND_FLOOR` to full at `BLEND_OK`, and leads its reasons ("Parfumo voters layer Layton with Althaïr"). A pair off for the weather (fit below `BLEND_OFF`, the pairs that say "Too rich for this heat") sorts after every pair that isn't (`byPick`), votes or not. A note listed in over a third of the collection (bergamot, musk and sandalwood in the demo, `commonNotes`) doesn't count as a shared-note tie. The boost sits on top of the model: `rank` orders picks and can pass 1 to break ties among pairs the model already rates perfect, `score` stays capped at 1, and partner averages leave the boost out. The hand-set rules don't predict the votes (on the wardrobe's 21 voted pairs in October 2026 the model alone ranked them at chance, AUC 0.47), so treat the votes as the better evidence when tuning `PAIRS`.

`planWindows` plans several windows in time order (Now's day + night, the Week tab, `--week`): each pick counts as a `planned` wear for later windows, and a bottle not yet in the plan takes the slot when it scores within `PLAN_MARGIN` (exported, 0.06) of the top, unless he rates it 2★ or lower (`DISLIKED`). A repeat that tops the window anyway (a 1-bottle list, or nothing else within reach) is shown without the repeat penalty, so its match doesn't drop and "Already picked for that day" doesn't lead its reasons. A wear already logged for a window stays its pick. A window can carry its own occasion (`win.occasion`, a trip's formal night) over the plan's.

## Scent styles

`src/scent-styles.js` scores any engine record (wardrobe, catalog, `fromFragrantica`, hand-entered) 0..1 on the words people shop by: 22 scent styles (`kind: 'scent'`: Skin scent, Clean, Fresh citrus, Aquatic, Green, Barbershop, Gourmand, Vanilla, Amber, Oud, Leather, Tobacco, Incense & smoke, White floral, Rose, Floral, Powdery & iris, Woody, Spicy, Fruity, Chypre & mossy, Boozy) plus Intimate (`kind: 'wear'`), read from voters' sillage and ranked apart, so a quiet bottle still shows its character. `STYLES` carries each one's label, tooltip `blurb` and scoring tables.

A style's score is a noisy-OR of its accords' strengths (`acc`; strength^1.3, so an accord has to lead to count fully) plus a noisy-OR of its notes' salience (`notes`: votes over the bottle's most-voted note, the pyramid tier when it has none; a third item is a floor for a defining molecule like Calone), damped by exp(-penalty) for accords that pull away from it (`against`: vanilla isn't Fresh citrus), then eased toward 1 above 0.8. A few styles add structure: fougère (lavender with coumarin or moss), chypre (citrus over moss and patchouli, and a dry bergamot-patchouli "modern chypre" without moss), laundry (fresh air over musk) and soapy iris (iris with neroli, unless amber or vanilla warms it) for Clean, and Amber only as warm as its resins (ambroxan's "amber" in Sauvage isn't). Skin scent and Clean lean on sillage a little. Hand-entered bottles score on accords alone and never wear close.

The rules that turn scores into labels read a scores object, so scores stored ahead of time show and count like live ones: `topOf(scores)` gives the scent styles worth showing (from `STYLE_MIN` 0.55, strong from 0.75, 3 at most), `closeOf` the Intimate flag (from 0.6: the quietest 15% of well-voted bottles) and `leanOf` what a bottle leans toward (its top 2 from 0.4). What a card or page shows is `shownOf(scores)`: `topOf`, or, when no style clears 0.55, the best one within `LEAN_SHOWN` (0.1) as a lean ("Leans skin scent", `leans: true`: Narciso Rodriguez For Her, Guerlain Vetiver, Kouros, Bleu de Chanel EDP; of the pool's 1,000 most voted, 29 showed no style and 2 still don't). Logic (tastes, By style) reads `topOf`. `topStyles(record)` (`shownOf`, or `topOf` from a `min` given) and `wearsClose(record)` score a record and apply them, and `styleMix(records[, scoresOf])` counts a collection's leans and who wears close. Don't re-derive these rules elsewhere. Accords decide where they disagree with reputation: Daisy is a white floral, Clean Warm Cotton aquatic-citrus. Boozy and Incense lean on notes, so a bottle without a liquor or incense note misses (Khamrah). A record scores in about 10 µs (tables compile to slots, note names' matches are cached): 10,000 dataset bottles take 100–160 ms in Node or desktop Chrome, about 4 times that on a phone; `topOf` and `closeOf` on 10,000 stored scores take about 15 ms.

After changing weights: `npm test` (`test/scent-styles.test.js` wants at least 90% of the canonical bottles in `test/style-exemplars.js` and 80% of the held-out ones to show their style in their top 2, pins the wardrobe and a few boundary bottles, and fails when an accord in `src/accords.js` sits in no style's `acc` or `against`), and `node scripts/check-styles.mjs` for the misses. Run `--save /tmp/before.json` before the change and `--against /tmp/before.json` after: it counts the shown styles gained and lost per style and lists every well-voted bottle whose lean, shown styles or Wears close changed, with old and new scores. Read them all, and eyeball more with `--sample 50` or `--style <id>`. Tune on the exemplars only: a bottle you tune on moves from the held-out set into the exemplars, and an untuned one takes its place.

## Candidates and similarity

The gap finder and Discover pick from the suggestion pool with two pure modules; build on them rather than re-deriving any of it. `src/similar.js` says how alike two bottles smell: `scentVector(record)` is a pair of unit vectors, accords by Fragrantica strength and the 8 strongest notes by votes (top notes count 0.6: the drydown is most of the wear; only 8 so an owned bottle's full pyramid compares with a pool row's), cached per record. `similarity` is 0.6 × the accords' cosine + 0.4 × the notes', accords alone when either has no notes: Liquid Brun and Althaïr 0.92, Club de Nuit Intense Man and Aventus 0.78, Atlantis Extrait and Liquid Brun 0.12. `centroid` blends several bottles into one target (when some have notes and some don't, a hand-entered seed beside a dataset one, a bottle without notes counts its accords in their place and the target carries the accords' `share`, so likeness to it ranks as the mean likeness to each: Aventus and a hand-entered rose-oud give oud and amber bottles, not only Aventus-alikes), `nearestOf` finds the closest in a list, and `sharedTraits` names what two share for copy ("bourbon vanilla, sweetness, warm spice, praline"; accords come as the engine's nouns, `accordNoun` in `src/accords.js`). In `sharedTraits` a note folds into the one-word accord its last word names (Bourbon Vanilla into vanilla, White Musk into musky, Aldehydes into aldehydic) but never on a kind like Notes or Leaf, and a name that repeats one listed before it is left out ("fresh" beside "fresh spicy").

Similarity misses some voted dupes (Supremacy Collector's Edition and Absolu Aventus 0.31), so `src/candidates.js` reads the "smells like" votes too. Its signals come from `extras`, which only the pool's records and the built demo, catalog and originals carry: a visitor's bottles and share-link ones load from r/ without them. So every function that reads them takes `byId` (the pool, id -> fromPool record; `allowContext(owned, { byId })` passes it on) and reads the bottle's pool row instead, found by id or, for an old `fg:` or hand-entered id, by name and brand (`poolRowOf`); outside the pool, its `about` (loadAbout: value, lean, flags and smells-like links, with decant.py's thresholds; no safety, keep or cheaper twins). Attach `about` to a visitor's bottles outside the pool before asking. `signalsOf(record, byId)` gives them in one shape (string ids; `known: false` when none of the three has anything, as for a hand-entered bottle or one newer than the dataset). `familyIndex(byId, owned)` links bottles by their family links both ways, counting each dupe's `original` as owned, and `dupeOf(record)` places a bottle: `original` of an owned dupe or `near` (a twin of an owned bottle or original), ×0.15 novelty; `flanker` (same brand and line, and similarity 0.3+: Supremacy Silver is one, Supremacy Noir a new direction), ×0.3; `family` (two family links, or a plain `like` link), ×0.6. For David the pool holds 79 near, 18 flankers and 78 family; Club de Nuit Intense Man is family "through Aventus". Only built dupes have an `original`, so from r/ an original counts as a twin where voters link it (17 of David's 19) and the family reaches one hop less. `novelty(record, ownedVectors(owned, byId), family)` is 0.35 + 1.6 × (1 − similarity to the nearest owned bottle or original), at most 1, times that multiplier; `ownedVectors` takes originals from the pool, since a built `original` has no accords or notes. It compares with every owned bottle (300 ms for the whole pool against David's 44), so take it lazily, for candidates that could still win.

`allow(record, allowContext(owned, { byId }))` drops owned bottles, owned dupes' originals, brigaded ones, body mists (unless `allowContext(…, { mists: true })`), ratings under 3.8 and leans outside the wardrobe's 10th–90th percentile ± 0.4 (lean runs -1..1; under 3 bottles with a known lean, any). A mist is the `mist` flag: a body or hair mist, body spray or splash by its name, its page ("Fine Fragrance Mist by", "this body mist") or a members' summary point that calls it one ("impressive longevity for a body mist"; not "performs like a body mist"), 80 pool bottles (Bath & Body Works 31 of 76, Phlur's Fragrance Mists, Sol de Janeiro's Cheirosas, Lush body sprays, Sauvage Very Cool Spray), never Cashmere Mist or Iris Silver Mist. A name counts only when it says what kind ("Body Mist", "All-Over Spray", "Very Cool Spray", "Brume Soyeuse pour le Corps", "Brume de Bain"): a plain "spray" or "brume" is a perfume's (Eau de Parfum Spray, On The Go Spray, Bon Parfumeur's 801 sea spray, Guerlain's Brume d'Automne), which flagged 65 dataset perfumes before. Pages that never say so slip through (Bath & Body Works' Warm Vanilla Sugar, Victoria's Secret's Bare Vanilla), so name the house when one comes up. Cheaper twins skip mists too. `candidateQuality` multiplies the rating (shrunk by 1,000 votes toward the pool's median), `keep`, value and reach, value by `valueSlope(owned, norms, byId)`: 0.19 for David's 4.2 median against the pool's 3.0, 0.08 for Brooke's; `preferValueDefault` is on from a median of 3.8. Both need 3 bottles with a known value (else 0.08 and off). `cheaperTwin(record, byId, ctx)` takes the first `extras.alt` in the pool that rates 3.8+ and isn't brigaded or discontinued (Aventus: Club de Nuit Intense Man); given an `allowContext`, a twin the wardrobe owns comes first (Althaïr: "You own its twin, Liquid Brun."). `riskOf(record, byId)` scores what makes a blind buy risky (brigaded, not a blind buy, polarizing or a low safety percentile, owners letting it go, discontinued, reformulated, hard to find, longevity in the pool's bottom tenth): high from 3 points (7% of the pool), medium from 2, a single minor reason stays low but is listed, and a bottle with no crowd data is `unknown` ("There’s no crowd data on it yet.", or "No safety or value votes on it yet." when it has a rating), never low. The members' summary's "not a safe blind buy" counts 2 points, but 1 where the crowd's own safety percentile is 65 or more (`SAFE_TRUSTED`): Terre d'Hermès (82) and Dior Homme Parfum (94) are medium risk, not Risky. `major` says one reason counts double on its own (brigaded, not a blind buy, polarizing, very few love it). Over the 9,644-bottle pool in Node: vectors 76 ms, `familyIndex` 30 ms, `poolNorms` 13 ms (after `fromPool`'s 200 ms).

Reach (`reach` on the pool's signals) is how much a bottle's fans also like the pool's 5% most-owned bottles (its also-liked list). The pool's median is 0.85; Granado, Phebo, Natura and O Boticário's fans like each other's bottles (0.2), as do some indie houses' and Louis Vuitton's. A house's best known bottles reach further than the house (Granado's Bossa 0.40, Phebo's Limão Siciliano 0.55), so a bottle's reach is at most its brand's median plus 0.15, for brands with 5 pool bottles or more (`BRAND_REACH_SLACK` in `decant.py`; Granado's median is 0.05, Phebo's 0.11, niche houses 0.65 to 0.95, Louis Vuitton's 0.20, which caps at no damping). Under 0.35 a bottle counts for up to 20% less (`reachMult`; √ of that in Discover): Phebo's Entrelaço and Granado's Íris and Figo (0) leave the top of the quiet-style lists, Bossa falls from first to eighth in Intimate and Limão Siciliano from fourth to fifth; Riiffs' Zenith (0.28) loses about 4% and stays David's first gap pick. Owners (`owners`, Fragrantica's "have", kept to a quarter of a doubling in the pool) don't mark them: Granado has as many owners per rating as Lattafa, and a new release has few.

## Gap finder and buy check

For "what am I missing", "what should I buy next" or "I need more formal cold-weather scents": `node scripts/recommend.mjs --gaps [--city "<city>"] [--uses "office=often,formal=never"] [--value | --no-value] [--top N] [--json]`. It prints the wardrobe's strengths, a line on how much of what he wears has a strong pick, its gaps (gaps and thin spots first, then room to grow) and the bottles that would fill them, each with the situations it fills ("Date Night on mild nights: 88%, over your Infini Rose at 81%"; a later pick names the earlier one it beats, "over Infinity For Men (suggested above)"), the owned bottle it's closest to, a cheaper twin and its blind-buy risk. Picks fill only the gaps listed, so a strong wardrobe gets two to four, and "Nothing else would fill these gaps by enough" is an answer, not a failure. Without a place it uses the default climate (the ten temperate cities); with one, that place's last two years (one archive call). How often he dresses for each occasion changes the answer most (Formal and Date Night settle most of his list), so ask when it matters and pass `--uses` (Never, Sometimes or Often against the default shares; Daily picks always count, and "everyday" still means Casual). "Prefer great value" is on for David (his median value is 4.2), which keeps picks to bottles voters value 3.8+; `--no-value` lets in Guerlain and Michael Malul. Body mists stay out unless `--mists`. Reply with the two or three gaps that matter and the picks (David's default report has two) with why, naming a cheaper twin when there is one. `--exclude` and `--demo` apply as for picks.

For "should I buy X" or "is X worth it": `node scripts/recommend.mjs --check "<name or Fragrantica id>" [--city "<city>"] [--style "skin,clean"] [--json]` gives a verdict (Strong fit, Good, Covered, Redundant or Risky) with two or three reasons, the situations it fills, the closest owned bottle, its risk and twin. It knows the styles David loves (`DEMO_LIKED`: Skin scent, Clean, Intimate; `--style` names others), so a skin scent that fills no gap is still Good while he owns under three ("More of the skin scents you like: you own 2.": Glossier You, Molecule 01, Xtra Milk, Clean Skin), and a classic in none of them that he's covered for is Covered ("It's fresh citrus and aquatic, none of the scent styles you like.": Acqua di Gio, Encre Noire, Dior Homme Intense 2011). Taste alone doesn't vouch past a warning that counts double (`riskOf`'s `major`: not a safe blind buy, divisive): Muscs Koublai Khan, Valaya and Chanel 1957 are Risky with the style line second. Nor does it vouch for a body mist, which suggestions leave out: Book Loft stays Covered for someone who likes Intimate, unless `--mists`. `--style liked` is his styles too. It looks in the suggestion pool first, then the whole dataset (signals from the bottle's about data there); a name matching several takes the most voted and says so on stderr, so give the brand or id when it's ambiguous. A bare number is read as a name when a well-voted bottle (the pool's) has it (1881, 4711, 212), and says so on stderr; write `id:1881` for the id. Relay the verdict and reasons, and add what the numbers can't know (price, whether he likes the notes).

`src/gaps.js` is pure; the pool worker (below) runs it off the main thread. A cell is a use × day or night × a feels-like band (`BANDS`: cold under 40°F, cool to 58, mild to 72, warm to 84, hot above), 55 in all. `USES` are Daily picks (Wear › Now) and the seven occasions, with default shares of wears (Daily 40%, Office 15%, Date Night 9%, Casual, Outdoors and Cozy 8% each, Night Out 7%, Formal 5%), a day/night split each and band leanings (Outdoors leans warm, Cozy cold). A cell's weight is the use's share × its slot's × how often the band comes up there: `climateSummary` turns a place's day and night windows into each slot × band's share and 6 real windows from it (`PER_CELL`; 4 moved the best bottle in 6 of 55 cells), about 4 KB, and `DEFAULT_CLIMATE` is the ten SEASON_CLIMATE cities' (`node scripts/fit-gaps.mjs --write`, reading `.cache/climate/*-dew-*.json`, which add dew point to fit-seasons' files). `fitTable` scores a bottle in a cell exactly as `rank()` would in those windows (`scoreOf` on the engine's own parts, averaged; `test/gaps.test.js` pins them to 1e-6). Every bottle, owned or not, is scored as `sameFooting` has it, on the pool's evidence: no Parfumo occasion votes, its 8 strongest accords (the dataset's limit; scraped pages show 10, which moved owned bottles' occasion fits by as much as the thresholds below); flat season votes read as none in the engine itself (`steadyShares`, see Engine), so the gap finder and the app's `rank()` agree on them. The reference is the suggestion pool within the wardrobe's lean range, brigaded bottles out. A cell's shortfall is how far the wardrobe's best, and 0.35 × its second best, sit under the mean of the reference's top 20; it's a gap when 5% of the reference beats the best or the top beat it by 6 points, thin under 2 strong bottles (the reference's 90th percentile) with the next best under the 85th (a runner-up just under the strong line is depth), plenty with 4 strong within 3 points of the top, room to grow when otherwise short by `ROOM_DEFICIT`, and off for a use set to Never. `gapSummary` lists every gap, thin and room cell that comes up, neighbouring bands merged ("Formal on cool and mild nights"), gaps and thin first, then room to grow, each most severe first (weight × shortfall); `overview` says how much of what he wears has a strong pick; `strengths` names each use's deepest plenty run, and both ends' leaders when they differ. Reason and gap text never holds a temperature. The casual occasion is labelled Casual (it was Everyday) so it never clashes with Daily picks.

`suggest` is greedy: each round takes the pool bottle whose fills that matter most cut the shortfall in the listed cells, × `candidateQuality` × `novelty` × its risk (none for high, 0.85 for medium, 0.9 per listed reason) × its lean (2.5 × less the further it sits outside the wardrobe's own 10th–90th percentile, `LEAN_PAST`: at 1 × fresh men's bottles filled Brooke's gourmand wardrobe's office days, three of five more masculine than anything she owns), counts it as owned and goes again: a cell it lifts off the list stops counting, and a later pick's fills are measured against it. Kin of an earlier pick count as twins. Left out: what `allow` drops, owned bottles' twins and originals (`dupeOf`), discontinued and high-risk bottles, anything 0.8+ alike to an owned bottle (`SAME_SIM`; Sauvage EDT and EDP are 0.80), and bottles with no fill that matters. A fill matters (`FILL_MARGIN`) when it lifts the best by 2 points, or adds a strong pick to a thin cell, but not when it's kin of the bottle it backs up (0.75 alike, `KIN_SIM`, or linked by voters: a Cream Velvet look-alike adds no depth to Cream Velvet), and not for Office, Date Night, Night Out or Formal when it fades fast (`weakLongevity`: Book Loft and Snowflakes & Cashmere won Office for being quiet before the engine counted fading against them; the guard still holds where a thin wardrobe leaves a weak bottle on top). `buyCheck(record, ctx, { liked })` scores a bottle as its pool row when it has one, so a catalog record and its pool row check alike, and decides in order: Redundant only when it duplicates something owned (owned, an owned dupe's original or twin, 0.8+ alike, or 0.75+ alike and placed in the family by voters: "Voters call it a twin of your Supremacy Collector's Edition Pour Homme"); Risky when `riskOf` says high; Covered when nothing it adds matters and it brings no liked style the wardrobe is thin on, saying where it comes closest to the wardrobe's best and who covers that ("Your Sticky Fingers outdoes it even for Formal on cool nights, where it comes closest.", or "It would barely beat your Terra for …, and nowhere by more."), then why it adds no taste ("It's woody, none of the styles you like."; beside a liked Intimate, "none of the scent styles you like", and "It's woody and wears close, but none of the scent styles you like." for a quiet one; or "You own 8 vanilla scents already.") and whether anything owned smells like it; Risky when voters rate it under 3.8 (the pool's bottom fifth); Strong fit when it cuts the listed gaps by `STRONG_GAIN` and by as much as the pool's top 2% would for this wardrobe (`STRONG_TOP`; else a third of the pool is a strong fit for a thin wardrobe), with quality at the pool's usual or better and low risk, never for a body mist or a bottle without crowd data; else Good: it fills something, or it's more of a style you like that the wardrobe holds under `STYLE_THIN` (3) of (`tasteOf`: one of its two shown scent styles; Intimate counts alone only when no scent style is liked, since a quiet aquatic isn't the skin scent someone means). A body mist says so ("It's a body mist: lighter and shorter-lived than a perfume."). Every suggestion checks Strong fit or Good. Over the pool for David: without his styles 2 strong fits, 239 good, 8,546 covered, 148 redundant, 709 risky; with them 478 good, and low-rated skin and clean scents move from Covered to Risky. `checkJSON` carries `taste` ({ styles, style, label, owned, thin }) for the UI; the worker's check takes `liked`.

David in the default climate (October 2026): his wardrobe covers every situation well; strong on the office any day (his fresh clones last the working day), cold and cool date nights, nights in general and cool-weather outdoors; room to grow on Date Night on mild and warm nights (Infini Rose and Supremacy Collector's Edition, just short of a gap: the top bottles beat Supremacy by 5.9 points, the line is 6, and it was a gap while Shalimar Millésime Tonka's flat votes made it a warm-night bottle), Cozy on mild nights and Daily picks on hot nights. Value picks: Riiffs Zenith (mild and warm date nights, mild cozy nights) and Armaf Infinity For Men (hot nights), then "Nothing else would fill these gaps by enough"; without the value filter, Zenith and Michael Malul Ocean Noir. Houston adds formal in the heat (Assaf Arrogate Pink), Minneapolis formal nights (Ahmed Al Maghribi Blu Oud). Without its 8 fresh bottles the wardrobe's top gaps become warm and hot days and fresh bottles the first picks (Missoni Wave, Agua Fresca Citrus Cedro; tested).

In the app the pool client asks `report` (`gapReport` as plain data: the coverage grid with each cell's status, the overview line, gaps, strengths, and picks with their records, fills, twins and risk) and `check` (`checkJSON(buyCheck(…))`), attaching about data to a visitor's bottles outside the pool (see "The pool client"). `summary: null` means the default climate, and `uses` (the "How you wear" settings) can stand in for `shares`; shares that aren't numbers fail. The first report takes about 0.5 s from page load in desktop Chromium and 2.7 s at 4× CPU, less once `warm` has worked out the pool's table for the default climate in slices while nothing else is asked (`warmPoolTable`: in Node a first report goes from about 0.37 s to 0.13 s; a local climate still works its table out on the first report); other shares or the value toggle take about 0.05 s, and a check a few ms. `dev/gaps.html` prints a report through the client, and `?inline` runs it on the main thread, where DevTools' CPU throttling applies (it doesn't reach workers). `fetchClimate(lat, lon)` in `src/climate.js` makes the one archive call (hourly feels-like and dew point, the last two full years, about 350 KB) and keeps the summary in localStorage per half degree (`scentcast.climate`), falling back to `DEFAULT_CLIMATE`. `src/uses.js` keeps "How you wear" (`scentcast.uses`: Never 0, Sometimes 1, Often 2.5 × each default share, renormalized; Daily always on), and `sharesFrom(settings, counts)` blends journal occasion counts in as (share × 20 + count) / (20 + n) once wears record an occasion. Show status words, titles and a use × band grid, not the severity numbers.

## Discover

`src/discover.js` finds bottles to buy that give more of what someone loves, from the suggestion pool and through the candidate machinery (filters, quality, novelty, family; see above). Pure, so it runs in a module Worker. `discoverContext(pool, owned, { styles })` works out what every query shares once (the pool's records, the wardrobe's filters, family index, owned vectors and lean: about 240 ms in Node or a desktop Chrome Worker, most of it `fromPool`); a query then takes 2–16 ms (the first "more like" also builds the pool's vectors, 70–85 ms, and the first notes search its note index, about 50 ms). Every pick comes as { record, score, styles (shown chips), close, nearest (the owned bottle it's closest to, `through` an owned dupe's original, and what they share), family (a smells-like link to the wardrobe), clonesOf (what a clone copies, going by the votes: `originalFromVotes`; `addCloneVotes(pick, about)` adds the votes), value, rating, risk, twin (a cheaper twin), alike (twins of it that also qualified, most voted first, folded in so a clone and its original take one slot) }, and `whyLine(pick)` says it in one line ("voters say it smells like Ultra Male (2851 to 175); closest you own: Hawas Elixir (through its original, Le Male Elixir: vanilla, sweet, tonka bean)"). Nothing owned, no owned dupe's original, no twin or version of an owned bottle and nothing over 0.9 alike to one is suggested (notes search shows them only with `excludeOwned: false`, labelled), and a bottle voters link to an owned one counts 0.6 of its score. Brigaded and discontinued bottles stay out, and ratings under 3.8 or a style's own floor (below), unless the filters say otherwise.

Style and notes lists lean the way the wardrobe does: the best feminine, unisex and masculine picks (crowd lean under -0.25, between, over 0.25) take turns toward the wardrobe's share of each (`mergeTurns`; David's 17%, 36% and 47%), so a mostly masculine wardrobe asking for skin scents gets Narciso Rodriguez For Him Intense, Dior Homme 2020 and Bleu Noir beside Xtra Milk and Clean Skin rather than a list of women's musks (before: 10 of the top 12 feminine). Only picks scoring 0.6 of their style's best take turns, and beside Intimate only ones that wear close, so a thin band doesn't bring in bottles that barely match. A lean filter replaces the turns (pass `--lean` when David asks for a gender); "more like" follows its seeds instead, keeping to their leans ± 0.3 (`SEED_LEAN_MARGIN`): with the wardrobe's window, More like Aventus offered Secret Wish and L'Aventure Femme.

- **By style** (`moreOfStyles(ids, ctx, { mode, k, filters })`, `--style "skin,clean,intimate" [--all]`): any (a bottle shows at least one chosen style; the styles take turns so one can't fill the list; Intimate beside scent styles shapes them, a bottle that doesn't wear close keeping 0.6 of its match, so "skin, clean, intimate" gives quiet skin and clean scents rather than every quiet lemon cologne) or all (each where a card shows it, 0.55 and Intimate 0.6, blended by a soft minimum: Musc Outreblanc, Fleur de Peau, Clean Skin, Le Musc & La Peau), × √quality × novelty. When fewer bottles than asked are all of them, ones leaning toward a scent style (from 0.4) follow, their tag saying so ("Leans oud", `leans` on the pick) and the CLI how many are all of them: oud and aquatic at once is Oud Minérale alone. Every pick's `styles` lead with the chosen ones it shows. Quality and the rating floor are judged against the style's own median rating: voters rate skin, clean and intimate bottles lower (median 3.96 against the pool's 4.02; a quarter sit under 3.8), so their floor is about 3.74 and Missing Person and Not A Perfume can show. `--min-rating` (`filters.minRating`) sets one. Typed styles forgive case and plurals ("skin scents"), and musky, soapy and fougère name Skin scent, Clean and Barbershop. Pool scores come from `data/fragrantica/pool-styles.json` (`node scripts/fit-pool-styles.mjs --write`: each pool bottle scored on its full r/ row, styles from 0.3 as 0–100, 227 KB raw, 70 KB gzip). `loadPoolStyles()` turns down a file made for another pool or one it can't fetch, and `discoverContext` one whose `check` (`styleCheck`: a hash of how `src/scent-styles.js` scores every 50th pool row) differs, as a service worker's old copy would; the queries then score live. Rerun it after changing `src/scent-styles.js` or remaking the pool; `test/discover.test.js` re-scores every pool row and fails until you do. The chips start on `likedStyles(owned, { stated })`: the styles the most bottles lean toward, or, given `stated`, those of the stated styles the wardrobe holds a bottle of. David's mix leans vanilla, citrus and spicy, and the styles he says he loves are his thinnest (Skin scent: Sandalwood & Musk, Meant To Be Seen; Clean: Cedrus Blanc; Intimate: Jake, Shiyaaka Sky), so the demo and the CLI pass `DEMO_LIKED` (`--style liked`, which falls back to the mix when none is left). Someone's own choice, once saved, wins.
- **By example** (`moreLike(seeds, ctx, { k, filters })`, `--more-like "Liquid Brun,Amber Empire"`): nearest to the seeds' centroid (owned or any dataset bottle; a seed without accords, like a dupe's built `original`, stands in as its pool row) × √quality, and a bottle voters link to an owned one counts 0.6, so "more like Sauvage" gives Dylan Blue and Terre d'Hermès rather than Salvo Intense. "Fans also like" lifts a pick up to 15% (`ALSO_LIKED`) by the share of seeds whose about data lists it in `alsoLiked`; give dataset seeds their `about` (`loadAbout`) for that. It's a small prior with no clear measured effect: holding each of David's 20 pool bottles out and seeding with its three nearest owned bottles moved mean reciprocal rank from 0.109 to 0.111, within noise; at 40% the best placed start to slip (Détour Noir from 1st to 3rd). `defaultSeeds(owned, { history, todayISO })` gives the three most worn of the last 60 days, else the best rated: your own stars where you gave some, the crowd's otherwise; a bottle you rate 2★ or lower (`DISLIKED`) is never a seed (`--more-like favorites`, with `--journal` for the wears and stars).
- **By notes and accords** (`searchNotes(query, ctx, filters, { k })`, `--notes "iris leather"`): the longest runs of words that name an accord, a note or an alias ("tonka bean", "lily of the valley", "white floral") are terms, every one must match, and a bottle scores the geometric mean of each term's strength (its accord's strength, or its notes' share of the votes) × √quality. Case, accents, plurals and adjective endings don't matter ("Irises"; smoke, smoky and smokey). `SYNONYMS` finds a note under any of its names (oud, agarwood and oudh; iris and orris; tobacco and tabac; leather, suede and cuir; sandalwood and sandal wood), and `BROADER` lets "fresh" and "warm" take any accord of the kind, so "marine fresh" is a fresh marine. A word that names nothing reads as the name one edit away (two from 7 letters; "lavendar", "tobaco", "sandlewood"; a four-letter one only through a swap of neighbours, "irsi" for iris, as any other edit turns too many short words into a note), reported in `corrected`; one that names only a note on under 100 bottles stays as typed, with a far commoner near name in `suggested` ("vanila" is a misspelt note on 43 bottles; clover isn't clove). A word near nothing returns no results and comes back in `unknown`.
- **Filters** (`FILTERS`, all three queries; the CLI's `--season`, `--slot`, `--lean`, `--min-value`, `--min-rating`, `--mists`): seasons (near the bottle's best season), day or night (night from 45% of its day/night votes; both read the split as `rank()` does, `steadyShares`), body mists (`mists`, off), gender lean (`LEAN_WINDOWS`: feminine, unisex or masculine, overlapping; without one, the wardrobe's window and the lean turns, or for "more like" the seeds' leans), longevity and sillage minimums on Parfumo's 0–10, value, safety, rating, discontinued, and owned (search only: style and example picks never include owned bottles or their twins).
- **The dupe explorer** (`dupesOf(record, ctx, { more })`, `--dupes "Aventus"`): { original, cheaper, siblings }. The original is an owned dupe's own (`built`), else `originalFromVotes`: the first bottle it smells like (family links, then plain ones) from another brand, released no later, valued 0.5 lower and, through a plain link, 0.3 alike; never for a bottle with no year. Cheaper is one list, most agreed and alike first (net votes × similarity): voters' cheaper twins (`alt`), for a bottle that isn't a dupe its clones (its `clones`, and pool bottles whose family links name it, from other brands, released no earlier, valued 1 higher and 0.3 alike), and up to 5 pool bottles 0.75 alike by notes and valued 1 higher that no vote links (`why: 'notes'`: Khamrah for Angels' Share); a voted one under 0.3 alike is marked `notesDiffer` (Cloud for Baccarat Rouge 540). Siblings are everything else voters link it or its original with, from 5 net votes and 2:1, most agreed first. Each entry has value, rating, risk, similarity and the smells-like votes its about data has. `dupeIds` lists what it looks at outside the pool; load those from r/ into `more`. Voters' links aren't all clones (Lalique White in Black for Layton), so say "voters say it smells like", not "clone of", outside the cheaper list.

`src/wishlist.js` keeps the want list in localStorage (`scentcast.want`): { id, added, note?, record? }, newest first, a full record only for a bottle the dataset can't load again by id (as "mine" keeps its records). `addWant`, `removeWant`, `hasWant`, `setWant` (toggle and save); a want without a record loads by its id.

The Discover screen (`#/discover`; `src/views/discover.js`, templates in `src/views/discover/templates.js`, its URL in `src/views/discover/query.js`) asks all of this through the pool client, one channel per list. Search reads names and notes at once (the dataset's name index and the pool's `notes`): the notes list shows when every word names a note or accord, unless a bottle named exactly that is one people mean by the words (`searchMode`): one on the device, one typed with its brand, or one with `WELL_KNOWN` (5,000) rating votes, which the pool's `records` tells it (Tobacco Vanille 31,666 and Oud Wood are names; "smoky vanilla", "amber vanilla" and "rose oud" stay notes though small houses named bottles that, Rose Oud by Yves Rocher at 1,963 votes). By notes / By name switches. By style starts on the styles hearted on Insights (`state.likedStyles`), else the ones last picked there (`scentcast.styles`), else `DEMO_LIKED` on David's wardrobe, else what most of the bottles lean toward; Any of them or All at once; holding or pointing at a chip says what the style is and which owned bottles are it. By bottle starts from the most worn of 60 days (else the best rated) and takes up to 5 bottles, owned or any from the library; a seed the library can't find (a link's hand-entered bottle) shows as Not found, with its X. The filters (season, time of day, lean, longevity and projection minimums on Parfumo's scale, "Prefer great value", safe blind buys, discontinued, and body mists, which stay out unless that's on) apply to all three lists. ## The pool client

Every screen asks the suggestion pool through `src/app/pool.js`: `askPool(type, { ...(await poolInputs()), … }, { channel })` resolves the answer, or rejects with an Error whose message can be shown as it is (a bug reads "Something went wrong working that out. Try again.", with the real message as `err.detail`). The questions are `src/pool-tasks.js`'s (listed in its header): `report` and `check` (the gap finder and buy check), `liked`, `styles`, `moreLike`, `notes` and `dupes` (Discover), `records` (engine records for ids: the pool's rows once it's in, else `r/`, never waiting for it; `poolOnly` waits and sends only pool rows, which is all Discover's name check needs, since every bottle with 500 rating votes is in the pool) and `warm`. Lists leave out the smells-like vote counts behind a pick's `clonesOf` (an `x/` shard per cloning pick, shown nowhere) unless asked (`cloneVotes: true`). `poolInputs()` (`src/app/state.js`) is what every question says about whoever asks, so Insights, a bottle page's buy check and the Wishlist agree: `owned` (`rankedFragrances()`: with his stars, so a bottle he rates 2★ or lower covers nothing on Insights), `exclude` (the list's bottles switched off: they cover nothing, but are never suggested back, nor their twins, originals or look-alikes, and checks and dupes call them owned; before, switching Cream Velvet off made a check call it a strong fit), `summary` (the place's climate, `localClimate()`, fetched once per place), `shares` ("How you wear" with the journal's occasions) and `preferValue` (`state.preferValue`, set with `setPreferValue`): true or false turns "Prefer great value" on or off, a hard floor at `PREFER_VALUE` (3.8) for the gap finder and Discover alike, null the wardrobe's default (on when its median value is 3.8 or more); the learned value slope always counts. Every bottle an answer names comes as its full engine record (`record`, ids as strings), so a screen can rank it, and `src/app/state.js` keeps those records (`keepBottles`), so `findRecord(id)` and the bottle page open any of them at once. A pool record has the pool's signals (`extras`) and its 8 strongest notes; `loadFullRecord(id)` swaps in the `r/` record's every note, keeping the signals.

The questions run in one module Worker (`src/pool-worker.js`, a shell around `poolServer`), started on the first ask and shared app-wide: `liked` and `records` (`QUICK`) at once, the rest one at a time in the order asked; where module Workers don't start (or aren't up within the timeout), the same server is imported and runs on the main thread (`inlineWorker`). A newer question on the same `channel` replaces the older one, which resolves `{ stale: true }` (drop it) and never runs if it hadn't started: give each list on screen its own channel (`disc-notes`, `ins-report`) so typing never shows an older answer. A question's clock (`TIMEOUT`, 60 s) runs from when the worker starts it and stops while the pool downloads, so a slow line never fails it; one past it is stuck, and fails alone while the questions behind it go to a fresh worker. A file that sends nothing for 30 s fails as out of reach. `poolStatus()` is idle, loading, ready or error, for skeletons; `warmPool()` starts the load when someone heads for Wardrobe or Discover. The worker keeps each wardrobe's gap and Discover contexts for the last two wardrobes (keyed on the records as they score, so a skin nudge or re-import makes a new one), so the value toggle or switching lists doesn't work them out again. The service worker keeps the pool's three files in a cache of their own (`scentcast-pool`), so the shards bottle pages and dupes read (`x/`, `r/`) can't push them out and leave the gap finder and Discover dark offline. In desktop Chromium (`dev/pool.html`, which times every question): a fresh worker's first answer takes 0.48 s for a report, 0.46 s for a check and 0.2–0.23 s for Discover's questions (worker start, the pool, the context), and later ones 1–10 ms (median; notes and dupes 40 ms the first time, building their indexes), with the main thread never held up; run on the main thread, the first report blocks it for 0.4 s. With the pool's files 3 s late, `liked` answers in 30 ms.

## The bottle page

Any bottle opens a page (`#/bottle/<id>`: a sheet on phones, a drawer on tablets, an inspector beside the page from 1100px) ranked against the list it came from (a bottle ranked on its own counts your stars, `applySkin`). Besides its reasons, layering, accords, seasons and notes, it has a section per question, each in its own module (`src/views/bottle-*.js`, each registering its `order` slot with `addSection`), each drawing nothing when it has nothing to say and a skeleton while it loads. For a bottle you don't own the page reads in shopping order (`shopOrder`): the buy check, how it would do now, its style, accords and notes, what it smells like, what voters say and their pros and cons, its seasons, then spraying and layering last.
- For a bottle you don't own, the buy check (`bottle-check.js`): the pool's `check` against the ranked list with `poolInputs()` and the styles you like (`likedStyleIds`, as Insights reads them: hearted, David's `DEMO_LIKED`, else what the bottles lean toward), so it agrees with Insights and the Wishlist: the verdict (any string the gap finder returns), its reasons, what it would fill grouped by use ("Date Night · mild and warm nights"; for a good fit they lead), "A style you like" ("A style you reach for", and "…scents you reach for" in its reasons, when the styles are only the bottles' leanings: it sends `stated`, which `buyCheck` takes), the closest bottle owned when it's 60% alike or voters link them, and a cheaper twin, which has its own Want heart. Each thing is said once: a reason the chips or rows repeat drops out. It sends the record whenever it has accords, so a catalog bottle newer than the dataset gets a check too; `src/pool-tasks.js` passes the liked styles to `buyCheck`, which (with the gap finder's taste check) turns "nothing to fill" into Good for more of a style you love.
- How much to spray (`bottle-spray.js`, `#bp-spray`): `sprayGuide` for the page's window and occasion, with your skin nudges, the spots lit on a small figure.
- On your skin (`bottle-skin.js`, owned or in Mine): your stars and whether it lasts or projects less or more on you (`src/skin.js`). Saving re-ranks (`app.render()`); a new rating says where the bottle moved in its list ("Now 23rd for tonight, was 1st." for a 1★ top pick; every list ranks `rankedFragrances()`, so it moves at once), a nudge the spray guide's new count; screen readers hear the line from `#bp-status`, a live region at the end of the body that outlasts the page's renders (one drawn anew with its text isn't announced). Tapping the checked star clears it; keys only choose, as on a native radio (Clear clears).
- Its style (`bottle-styles.js`): `topStyles` with each style's blurb, Intimate for quiet bottles (named as on its chip and Discover's cards; its blurb says it wears close), "You like it" on the styles you said you like (liked on Insights, or David's `DEMO_LIKED`), "You reach for it" when they're only what your bottles lean toward (`likedTaste`'s `stated`), each a link to Discover's style list (`#/discover?styles=<id>`). No heart: on the bottle page the heart means Want.
- Smells like (`bottle-dupes.js`, `#bp-dupes`): the pool's `dupes`: the original a dupe copies, bottles that smell like it for far less, and the ones voters link it with, with their votes ("2,813 say it smells like it"; the page's own top five when the pool has none, else "Voters link them") and value in words; each opens its page or a comparison, and the credit names Le Decanteur's dataset. Asking loads the whole pool (about 0.8 MB) and the section sits well below the fold, so it asks only when its slot comes within 400px of the panel's view (an IntersectionObserver; a jump to `#bp-dupes` counts), at once when the pool is already in; until then it's a skeleton. A bottle you own opened on its own never downloads the pool.
- What voters say (`bottle-crowd.js`): value for money, the share of owners who keep it, how safe a blind buy it is (only for a bottle you don't own; `riskLine`: "Safe bet" only from the pool's 50th safety percentile, else "Fair bet", a caveat after it, never a contradiction), how talked about it is now, the spread of its ratings, how it reads on a feminine to masculine scale (voters' perception, not who wears it), its perfumers and line; and Pros and cons (`bottle-reviews.js`), Fragrantica members' summary. Both credit Le Decanteur's dataset, and say so with Try again when the about data couldn't load (`state.aboutFailed`).
Each section sits in a slot (`<div class="bp-slot" data-section="<id>">`). An answer that lands draws only its section (`pooled(…, { section })` → `redrawSection`), and when one above the reader grows, what they're reading stays put; a whole-page draw keeps the section they were at in place too (`readingAt`, `keepReading`) and hands focus to the same control (links carry `data-focus`) or the element with its id, never the body.
The actions add Want (`src/wishlist.js`; the toast's "See it", and Undo when a bottle comes off; the page beside it draws too) and Compare, which opens "Compare with…" (five from the bottle's smells-like family, asked as it opens when the page hadn't yet, five from the list it came from, then all your other bottles, and a search of the library: every word typed, in any order, against name and brand, the library's own matches as it found them) and then the compare page: `#/compare/<id>,<id>[,<id>]` (`src/views/compare.js`), up to three bottles side by side. On top, tonight's pick: with a bottle you own among them it's yours, with both matches for tonight, as the rows may show another window ("Wear Détour Noir: The best of yours tonight, 87% match. Layton, which it's inspired by, matches 88%."), else the one that suits tonight best. Then their match now and for the saved occasion (bottles in Now's list read Now's entry, the rest rank alone with your stars), how they relate (Dupe, Voted, or "35% alike" by accords and notes), rating, accords, seasons (with their percents) and day/night, how long they last and how far they project (Parfumo's 0–10, or for a bottle Parfumo lacks "≈" an estimate on its scale from Fragrantica's votes, `performanceRating`, which never leads a row against Parfumo's own; the bottle page's head uses the same words, Lasts and Projects), value, styles and blind-buy risk. One leader per row is named, judged by the value as shown (a tie names none): "Best", and "Longest" and "Loudest" in neutral, as louder isn't better. The bottles stand in columns at every width under a strip of their photos that sticks once the cards scroll away; on phones the accords and seasons stack. One bottle shows an empty column offering its family; the bar has a Back to the bottle page it came from, a removal toasts Undo, a bottle that couldn't load has Try again, and the match rows and verdict show skeletons while the forecast loads. The page loads what it lacks itself: r/ records, about data and, for dataset bottles without them, the pool's signals.

## Insights and the Wishlist

Wardrobe › Insights is the gap finder and the style taste on screen (`src/views/wardrobe/insights.js`), Wardrobe › Wishlist the want list with its buy checks (`src/views/wardrobe/wishlist.js`); `src/views/wardrobe/parts.js` holds what they share. Neither loads `gaps.js`, `uses.js`, `discover.js` or `doubles.js` at boot: `loadLib()` imports them, with `scent-styles.js` (already in the boot graph for the spray guide, the bottle page and Compare), when either pane first opens. A failed import stays failed for the page's life (the browser keeps it), so `libFailed()` makes each pane say so once with a Reload button and ask nothing more; before, the pane re-rendered itself in a loop. Both ask the pool client with `poolInputs()` (so their verdicts agree with a bottle page's), keep the answers in module state and ask again only when `inputsKey()` changes (the ranked list, switched-off bottles, "How you wear", the journal's occasion counts, Prefer great value, the place), on channels named `ins-…` and `wish-…`; the styles half also checks the liked styles on every render, as Discover can change them.

Insights answers "what kinds of fragrance do I still need" in two halves. Situations: a headline from the report's overview ("You're covered for nearly everything you wear. Here's where it's thin."), the climate it read (the place's, or a typical temperate one with a note), How you wear (a sheet: Never, Sometimes or Often per occasion; it says when logged wears tune it), Prefer great value; then the coverage map, a table of the eight uses against the five feels-like bands by day and by night, each cell a vial filled as far as it's covered (plenty, covered, room to grow, thin; one the gap finder calls a gap, labelled "Falls short", has a coral rim and a mark at its top and fills as near as your best comes to the best out there, `vialLevel`, since a gap there means your best sits under the top 5% or 6 points or more under the best, not that you own nothing; struck through for Never, a dashed rim under 0.4% of wears), opening on the worst gap and showing a cell's best three bottles and the suggestions that would beat them; where it's thin (the four worst, the rest behind Show more) and where it's strong ("Plenty for Date Night on cold nights · Amber Empire and 6 more"); and the report's picks as cards, each fill drawn as a before-and-after on one track ("Date Night · mild nights 88%, over your Infini Rose at 81%"), with value in words (Great from 3.8, where Prefer great value draws its line), blind-buy risk ("Safe blind buy", "Try before buying"), the closest bottle owned, a cheaper twin, Want and I own it (only I own it's tag on one already owned). Styles: the wardrobe's style mix (`styleMix`, owned bottles only), each style with a Like toggle (the heart means Want everywhere); Like saves `state.likedStyles` in prefs (`[]` once every one is unliked; null: `DEMO_LIKED` on David's wardrobe, else what most bottles lean toward, `likedStyles`); a line on them against the shelf ("You like Skin scent, Clean and Intimate, but only 4 of your 24 bottles are.", `storyOf`); for each liked scent style three picks from the pool's `styles` (with Intimate beside it when liked, so they wear close) and a link to `#/discover?styles=skin,clean,intimate`, with a line saying when Prefer great value narrows them and a link to flip it; for each scent style with no bottle, one starter (`styles` with `{ minRating: 4, minSafe: 50, minValue: 3.5 }`; David's: Mercedes-Benz Le Parfum for oud, Burberry London for tobacco, Sultan Noir for incense, Pinaud Clubman for chypre); and doubles.

`src/doubles.js` finds bottles in one wardrobe that wear as one: 0.85 alike by notes and accords (`DOUBLE_SIM`; Sauvage EDT and EDP are 0.80), one the original the other copies, both dupes of one original, or a smells-like link either way, from either bottle or an owned dupe's original (Absolu Aventus 2023, Supremacy's original, links to Aventus, so the two are doubles). Each pair says why (`doubleReason`) and what the two share; with none, `closest` is the most alike pair (David: Détour Noir and Liam, 72%, vanilla, woody and warm spicy). `--doubles` prints the same. Smells-like links come from a record's signals, which only David's wardrobe and catalog records carry: for bottles added to Mine or opened from a share link (`r/` records), Insights first asks the pool for their rows (`warm`, then `records`, `needsSignals`) and passes them as `byId`, or Aventus and Club de Nuit Intense Man (76% alike by notes) read as no double.

The Wishlist checks every want at once (`check` with `records`, and the liked styles with `stated`, which the check task passes on to `buyCheck`, so styles only guessed from the bottles read as ones you reach for, as on a bottle page), then sorts them Strong fit, Good, Covered, Risky, Redundant, most gain first; each card has its two first reasons, what it would fill, its match in Now's window (ranked alone), I bought it (its full record into Mine through `app.own`) and Remove (with Undo, which puts focus back on the bottle). A want you already own (`ownedIn`) isn't checked: it says where it is ("In David's") and offers only Remove, last. A Redundant verdict that ties to nothing owned (no dupe or smells-like link, under `SAME_SIM`) shows as Covered (`verdictOf`), a safety net now that `buyCheck` says Covered itself. `wantButton` and the `wish-toggle` action are the Want button for any screen. The journal's Export now carries your ratings and skin nudges (`loadSkin()`), which `journal-check` and `--journal` read.

## Checking picks against real wears

The wear journal keeps a year of wears, each with roughly where it happened. Its Export button downloads them, with the device's place and hidden bottles. `node scripts/journal-check.mjs <export.json>` looks up each wear's weather in Open-Meteo's archive (the last five days, which the archive doesn't have yet, from the forecast API's `past_days`), ranks the demo as the app would have (string ids, like the app, so earlier wears count as rotation; the export's own ratings and skin nudges; the occasion a wear was logged for from Occasion; `rankWears`, tested), and reports where the worn bottle landed (average rank against random, top-pick and S/A rates). Wears whose weather didn't load (the forecast API has a daily quota) are skipped and counted. Use it before and after tuning the engine.

A wear records the use it was logged for (`toggleWear(id, date, slot, where, occasion)`: the occasion's id from the Occasion tab or a trip's event, `'daily'` from Now and the Week; the `wear` action reads it from the list's context), and `occasionCounts(history[, since])` counts wears per use for the gap finder's use shares (`{ daily, office, date, …, untagged }`). Wears logged before uses were recorded count as `untagged`, not daily, so a year of them doesn't swamp the shares; the gap finder reads only use ids. `journalExport(history, loc, hidden, { nowISO, skin })` adds David's ratings (`loadSkin()`) when there are any. Exports from before either change read the same way (`parseJournal`).

Dataset files (`data/fragrantica/`) answer from their cache at once and are checked behind it. It skips writing a file it already holds with the same ETag, and keeps one copy of the app page for every `?…` visit (other files opened in a tab skip it, so they can't stand in for the app offline); bump `CACHE` in `sw.js` when adding app files (`test/shell.test.js` fails until every file under `src/` is in `SHELL`, `index.html` preloads every module `main.js` imports, and links every stylesheet).
