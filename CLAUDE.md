# Scentcast

Picks fragrances from David's own Fragrantica wardrobe for the weather, time of day and occasion. Static web app: vanilla ES modules, no build step, no dependencies. The same engine runs in Node for answers in the terminal.

## Answering "what should I wear…"

1. Get live picks from the engine: `node scripts/recommend.mjs --city "<city>" [--occasion <id>] [--slot day|night] --json`. Without an occasion or slot it plans today's next two windows together, in time order, like the app's Today (each slot's `pick`, then the full `ranked` list). For "this week" or a trip, add `--week` for a day + night plan over the next 7 days (with an occasion, only its slot). `--occasion` takes an id or label in any case ("date night"); the ids are in `src/occasions.js`. Ask David for his city if he hasn't given it. Every wardrobe bottle counts, Jake included; `--demo` applies `DEFAULT_HIDDEN` like the site. Bottles he switched off in the app (Wardrobe › Owned) are stored on his phone only; pass any he mentions as `--exclude "Name,Name"` (part of a name is enough; it warns about misses). If he sends his journal export, `--journal <export.json>` rotates out what he wore recently, as the app does, applies his own ratings and skin nudges (see Engine), and turns on the rotation nudge. For a day further out ("Saturday's wedding", "a date on the 24th"), add `--date YYYY-MM-DD`: the forecast's windows when it reaches that far, typical weather for the date past it (the JSON says which). Every pick carries `spray` (see How much to spray).
2. For an occasion no preset covers (job interview, outdoor wedding, a flight), run the nearest preset(s), then adjust using `data/collection.json`: accord strengths (0–100), note pyramid, season and day/night vote shares.
3. Reply with a short ranked shortlist drawn only from `data/collection.json`: each pick with its reason (weather, votes, accords), plus a runner-up.

For "what temperature suits X": `node scripts/recommend.mjs --ideal "<name>" [--json]` gives the feels-like range it wears best in (`comfortRange` in the engine, also on its bottle page) and where it makes the wardrobe's top 3 by day and by night, whatever the time of year. A name that matches several bottles takes the first and says so on stderr.

For layering ("what goes with X", "cool layers"): `node scripts/recommend.mjs --layer "<name>" [--json]`, or `--layer all` for the best pairs in the wardrobe. No city needed; add `--city "<city>" [--slot day|night]` to score the pairs for that window's weather.

For styles ("what kind of scents do I own", "is X a skin scent"): `node scripts/recommend.mjs --mix [--json]` gives the wardrobe's style mix (how many bottles lean each way, which wear close) and each bottle's top two styles; `--styles "<name>" [--json]` gives one bottle's scores, from the wardrobe or, failing that, the Fragrantica dataset (see Scent styles).

For "what am I missing", "what should I buy" or "should I buy X": `--gaps` and `--check` (see Gap finder and buy check).

For buying ("more skin scents", "more like Liquid Brun", "something with iris and leather", "what smells like Aventus but cheaper"): `--style`, `--more-like`, `--notes` and `--dupes` (see Discover).

## Packing for a trip

For "what should I pack for London next week" or "3 days in Miami": `node scripts/recommend.mjs --pack --city "<city>" --from YYYY-MM-DD --to YYYY-MM-DD [--max N] [--occasion <id>] [--slot day|night] [--events "2026-10-20 night formal; 2026-10-21 date night"] [--json]`. It plans every day and night of the trip and packs the fewest bottles (up to `--max`, default 3) whose best for each window scores within `PLAN_MARGIN` of the whole wardrobe's top for it (`packTrip` in `src/pack.js`), at least two for a trip and three from a week, so there's something to trade off. It weighs every set of that size (a greedy cover missed two-bottle sets that covered a whole trip), and among sets that cover as much takes the one whose plan scores best, each pick less its rotation penalty: an extra bottle goes where it most improves the picks or spares a bottle worn day after day, not to a near copy of the first. Bottles David rates 2★ or lower only go in for a window nothing else suits. `--occasion` goes on every window of its slot (a work trip's days: `--occasion office`), `--slot` packs for days or nights only, and an event overrides both on its window: a date, an occasion (id or label) and an optional day or night before or after it ("night out" and "date night" are occasions; "night formal" and "formal night" a slot and one). The reply's `text` reads like "Pack Cream Velvet (every day), CH Men Prive (5 nights) and Sticky Fingers (4 nights and formal wear on Dec 15)"; each bottle's `why` leads with the windows it's worn and says where else it would do; `plan` says which to wear when (packed bottles that suit a window take turns by the rotation penalty), and `uncovered` lists windows nothing packed suits, with the bottle that would ("…; Terra would be, if there's room"). Lead with the text, then the plan.

Weather comes from `tripWindows` in `src/weather.js`: the forecast for `FORECAST_DAYS` (10 days with today), where at least two of the blend's models have a value, then typical weather (`typicalWindow`: the same dates, give or take three days, in each of the last three years Open-Meteo's archive has whole, averaged). Open-Meteo forecasts 16 days, but ICON stops after about 7 and GEM after 10; on `score-forecasts.py`'s airports typical weather missed window temperatures by 5.5°F, the blend by 3.6 a week out, GFS and ECMWF alone by 4.0 and growing half a degree a day, GFS alone by 5.0 (Open-Meteo keeps past runs only seven days out, so ten is that growth's reach). A typical window's `pop` is the share of those past dates with 1 mm of rain or snow in it (the archive codes a trace as drizzle), and its condition is a wet one only when most were. Typical windows carry `typical: true`; say so when you answer ("typical London weather for mid-December"). `picksFor` in `src/pack.js` ranks one date and slot the same way (`--date`), and `tripPlan` packs from a place and dates in the app. Trips run up to 31 days and a year ahead.

## How much to spray

`node scripts/recommend.mjs --spray "<name>" [--city "<city>" [--date YYYY-MM-DD] [--slot day|night]] [--occasion <id>] [--json]` gives a count and where for the next windows at a place, or for a hot sticky day, a mild day and a cold night without one. `sprayGuide(record, conditions, occasion, skin)` in `src/spray.js` starts from 3 and moves by sillage (1.5 sprays a level, `performanceLevel`, so skin nudges count) and longevity, the strength the name claims (Extrait, Parfum, Elixir, Intense, EDT, Cologne…; half as much once 50 voters rated its performance, since concentration doesn't decide sillage), heat and sticky air (fewer, low on the chest under a shirt unless the bottle is fresh and quiet; scaled by `heaviness`, so a gourmand loses nearly two sprays and a fresh citrus a quarter of one, since rich notes turn cloying and fresh ones burn off), cold (more, and on a collar or scarf, which holds it) and the occasion (Office at most 2, a date close and at most 4, a night out more). It returns `sprays: [lo, hi]` (1–6, one number when it lands near one), `where`, a one- or two-sentence `text` with no temperature, `why` and an optional `note`, the most useful one: a loud bottle at 1–2 sprays (you'll stop smelling it long before others do), one that fades early by the crowd's votes or David's skin, or a fresh one in heat (take it along to top up), or a close-wearing one (this many won't crowd anyone; never when it projects more on him). `test/spray.test.js` pins a table of cases collectors would agree with (a beast-mode extrait on a hot office day is 1; a light cologne on a cold night out 4–6; CH Men Prive on a hot sticky day 1–2, Essence de Blanc 2–3); add a case before retuning.

## Collection data

`data/collection.jsonl` is the raw scrape, one line per bottle. `data/collection.json` is generated from it by `npm run build:data`, so edit the jsonl.

To add or refresh a bottle:
1. Get its line. A bottle in the Fragrantica dataset (below; `python3 scripts/decant.py similar <id or "name brand">` finds it) needs no browser: `python3 scripts/decant.py add collection <id>` appends or replaces it. A bottle newer than the dataset needs the extractor: open its page with Claude in Chrome (David is logged in as @doeszen), run `scripts/fragrantica-extract.js` on the rendered page with the javascript tool (it returns one JSON line, clicking "Show votes" for the note votes, `noteVotes`, and back off), and append or replace that line in `data/collection.jsonl`.
2. `python3 scripts/decant.py extras`, `npm run build:data`, `npm run fit` and `npm test`.
3. If it's a dupe, add its original (below). Add its Parfumo line, and its original's (below).

`python3 scripts/decant.py refresh` takes newer vote counts from a new dataset version into every `data/*.jsonl` line, and rewrites `data/extras.jsonl` (below).

Dupes and their originals: `data/dupes.json` pairs a dupe's slug with its original's, and `data/originals.jsonl` holds the originals' lines (`decant.py add originals <id>`). The build gives each dupe a summary of its original's votes as `original`; the engine leans on it for a dupe's season and day/night split, the bottle page says "Inspired by", and `npm run fit` counts the originals as voted bottles. Find the original in the dataset's "smells like" votes: `python3 scripts/decant.py similar <id>` lists them, and the top bottle from another brand released no later is the curated original for 18 of the 19 dupes. `decant.py dupes` checks every pair against the votes and names unpaired bottles whose votes point to an original. When a dupe's list holds only fellow clones (Essence de Blanc's), run `similar` on one of them, or read the reviews: the raw HTML of a perfume page has its 200 newest `[itemprop="reviewBody"]`, and the most-named perfume after "dupe of", "clone of", "smells like" and the like is nearly always it. Ask David when the votes are split.

Longevity, sillage and layering votes come from Parfumo (Fragrantica's layout has none). `python3 scripts/parfumo.py` fetches them without a browser into `data/parfumo.jsonl`, one line per bottle by Fragrantica id: `path`, Parfumo id `pid`, `[average of 10, votes]` for scent, longevity and sillage, `layer`, the page's "Good to layer" list (`[Parfumo id, community consensus %]`, up to 30, best first), and `occasion`, its Occasion chart (% Daily, Leisure, Business, Night Out, Evening, Sport) with `occasionVotes`. `--search "<name brand>"` lists candidate paths, `--add <fragrantica id> <path>` adds a line, and no arguments refreshes every line; then `npm run build:data`. Requests go 2 seconds apart, and the script saves after each bottle: Parfumo's Cloudflare challenges a run partway (HTTP 403 on every page, for an hour or more), and it prints the command that resumes. A browser tab that has passed the challenge still fetches: in October 2026 the occasion charts came from the same requests run in a parfumo.com tab's console. Brand slugs vary (`Afnan_Perfumes`, `lattafa-pride`, `Goldfield__Banks`), names can differ (Atlantis Extrait is "Atlantis"), and Parfumo splits concentrations (Gris Charnel EDP and extrait), so check the match. The build joins the ratings as `parfumo` and the lists as `layeredWith` (see Engine). Originals get lines too: dupes have few layering votes of their own, and their originals hold most of the wardrobe's.

Bottles a first-time visitor never sees (demo-safe) are `DEFAULT_HIDDEN` in `src/hidden.js`. They only tidy the demo: a shared link or someone's own list shows them (`hiddenFor`). A device stores its own hides (`scentcast.hidden`, applied in every list) apart from the defaults it switched back on in the demo (`scentcast.shown`), so hiding a bottle in a shared link never drags a default along (`setHidden`).

`data/calibration.jsonl` (built to `data/calibration.json`) holds 52 well-voted bottles from across the accord families (Tobacco Vanille, Acqua di Gio, Angel, Terre d'Hermès…). Only `scripts/fit-accords.mjs` reads them, to fit accord heaviness on more than the wardrobe's own votes; the app never loads them. Add bottles with `decant.py add calibration <id>`, aiming for accords with few bottles behind them (the fit prints the count per accord).

The full owned list is the "Perfumes I Have" shelf at `https://www.fragrantica.com/@doeszen#wardrobe`. The profile home mixes in his Want list.

Fragrantica gotchas:
- The "When To Wear" and "Rating" cards render client-side from obfuscated embedded data. Read the rendered DOM (the extractor waits for it); raw HTML fetches lack those numbers.
- The current page layout has no longevity or sillage votes; those come from Parfumo (above), or the dataset's where Parfumo lacks a bottle.
- The javascript tool's output is cut at about 1,000 characters. For many lines, collect them in the page (localStorage), write them into a `<pre>` as the page body and read it with `get_page_text`. A script stored in localStorage runs on each page with `await new Function('return (async () => {' + code + '})()')()`. A javascript call over 45 seconds times out but keeps running in the page.
- Tool output that includes raw page HTML can trip a cookie/query-string filter; return parsed fields only.
- Fragrantica sometimes shows a Cloudflare "Verify you are human" check; ask David to click it.
- To find a bottle's page, open `https://www.fragrantica.com/search/?query=<name brand>` and read the `/perfume/` links once results render (the sidebar also lists David's own bottles).
- Bottle images hotlink `fimgs.net/mdimg/perfume-thumbs/dark-m.<id>.2x.webp` (transparent background).
- A brand page (`/designers/<Brand>.html`) lists its perfume links in the raw HTML, so `fetch` from a Fragrantica tab finds page URLs without the slow search. Fetch one at a time: a burst of about 40 got HTTP 429.
- Chrome throttles timers in a background tab, so client-rendered search results crawl there; perfume pages still render their cards.
- Newer pages use accords like cherry, tuberose, yellow floral, marine, salty, aldehydic, coffee, rum, smoky and patchouli. Each needs a row in `src/accords.js` and a `FAMILY_ALIAS` in `src/layering.js` (the family it layers like); `test/accords.test.js` names any that are missing.

## Fragrantica dataset

`data/fragrantica/` holds every Fragrantica perfume with accords (137,437), from Le Decanteur's Kaggle dataset (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0: the app's footer always credits it, since even the demo's records carry values derived from it, and `data/fragrantica/README.md` carries the license). The app searches it after the demo and catalog, and share links resolve its ids, which are Fragrantica's like the demo's. `src/fragrantica.js` turns a row into an engine record (`source: 'fragrantica'`), loading one search-index file per query and one record file per added bottle.

`scripts/decant.py` builds it (file layout and keys in its docstring). For a new dataset version: `python3 scripts/decant.py download` (needs David's Kaggle token in `~/.kaggle/access_token`), `build`, then `python3 scripts/decant.py extras`, `node scripts/fit-profiles.mjs --write`, `node scripts/fit-occasions.mjs --write`, `node scripts/fit-pool-styles.mjs --write`, `npm run build:data` and `npm test`. The build rewrites most of its 1,800 files, about 32 MB gzipped. `decant.py pool`, `about` and `extras` remake one piece each (every command reads the whole 555 MB dump, about 40 seconds); none of them touch `r/` or `i/`.

Beyond the search and records, the dataset has what a bottle's page shows and signals derived from its votes:
- `pool.json` and `pool-notes.json`: the suggestion pool for gap filling and Discover, 9,644 bottles: 500+ rating votes, plus rising ones (100–499 votes, recent popularity 1,000+: the new Middle-Eastern releases David buys). Columnar, one array per key, rows by brand then name (sort by `rv` for most voted). It holds everything the engine scores on, exact but for rating, Fragrantica longevity and sillage and the like-shares (2 decimals; `rank()` scores stay within 0.005 of the `r/` record's), and the signals below. `pool-notes.json` holds each bottle's 8 strongest notes, with votes in tenths of the top note's. 1.7 + 0.4 MB raw, 568 + 151 KB gzip -9, over the 600 KB aimed for; `test/pool.test.js` holds them under 730 KB together. To trim: 6 notes instead of 8 (about −28 KB), season and time votes to 2 significant figures (−25 KB) or bar widths to even numbers (−10 KB), the last two at some cost to scoring exactly like `r/`. `loadPool()` in `src/fragrantica.js` fetches both once, next to the module, so a module Worker under `src/` can call it (load it when a feature needs it, never on boot) and `fromPool(pool, i)` makes bottle i's engine record (`source: 'fragrantica'`) with the signals as `extras`.
- `x/<id % 512>.json`: the about data of the 48,291 bottles with 30+ rating votes, 13.8 MB raw, 6.0 MB gzipped. `loadAbout(id)` reads one with readable keys: `sentiment` (the rating histogram), `value` {avg, votes}, `owners` {have, had, want}, `crowdGender` {lean, votes}, `popularity` {all, recent}, `perfumers`, `line` (Fragrantica's collection), `pros` (top 3) and `cons` (top 2) as {text, up, down}, `smellsLike` (top 5 {id, up, down}), `alsoLiked` (10 ids: co-preference, "fans also like", not smells-like), `flags`.
- `data/extras.jsonl` (`decant.py extras`): the same for every bottle in the wardrobe, catalog and originals that the dataset has (one newer than it has neither `about` nor `extras`, so treat both as optional), plus their signals and `clones` (up to 6 bottles from other brands, released no earlier, valued 3.8+, that voters say smell like it: 20+ net votes, 2:1, either way round). The build joins it like `parfumo.jsonl`: the signals as `extras`, the rest as `about` (and its histogram as `sentiment` where a line has none, as `decant.py add` lines don't). A dupe's `original` gets the original's `extras`, `parfumo` and `performance`, and `siblings`, its other clones (Cream Velvet's Bianco Latte: Lattafa Eclaire and four more), since the app never loads `originals.json`.

The signals, on pool and wardrobe records alike (`extras`):
- `value`: value for money, 1 overpriced to 5 great value, few votes leaning to 3.1. It matches the AI summaries' "affordable" and "overpriced" at AUC 0.98 and barely tracks the rating. The pool's median is 3.0 ("Great value" from 4.0, "Pricey" at 2.4 or less); the wardrobe's is 4.2, so value is a taste for David, not just a badge.
- `safe`: blind-buy safety, the pool percentile (0–100) of the like + love share less 4 × the hate share. The hate share predicts an AI "not a safe blind buy" far better than the rating does.
- `keep`: the share of owners who keep it, over what pages that old keep (+0.1 or more: people keep it).
- `lean`: the crowd's gender vote, −1 feminine to +1 masculine. A soft taste signal, never a filter: David owns crowd-feminine bottles.
- `now`: the pool percentile of recent popularity (about two years).
- `like`: up to 3 bottles voters say it smells like (5+ net votes, 2:1). `family`: the ones among its top 6 voters call one scent (30+ agreeing votes, 2.5 × the disagreeing: dupes, clones, twins), for telling a near-copy of an owned bottle from a new direction. Links run one way; an owned bottle's own `about.smellsLike` (with votes) and `extras.family` cover the other. `alt`: up to 3 cheaper alternatives, for bottles valued 2.6 or less: smells-like either way round (10+ net votes, 2:1), valued 1+ higher (Aventus → Club de Nuit Intense Man; it finds 12 of the wardrobe's 19 dupe pairs on its own).
- `flags`: `discontinued`, `reformulated`, `notBlindBuy` and `hardToFind` from AI summary cons more voters agree with than not. `discontinued` counts only a con said of the bottle itself: pros praise "an alternative to the discontinued X", and cons like "weaker than the discontinued EDT", "may be discontinued" or "discontinued in some regions" don't count (`says_discontinued` in `decant.py`); "extremely difficult to find in stock" is `hardToFind`. `brigaded`: more than 1.5 ratings per owner with a lopsided spread (30%+ hate or 85%+ love), or more than 5 whatever the spread: SpongeBob, Bolsonaro, the Pride editions, Sauvage Eau Forte (leave these out of suggestions). Louis Vuitton bottles people rate from a tester run 1.5 to 2 ratings per owner with an ordinary spread, so the ratio alone isn't enough (Pur Ambre, loved by 87%, still gets flagged). `polarizing`, a hate share in the pool's top tenth.

The pros and cons are Fragrantica's AI summary of its members' reviews. Quote them as "Fragrantica members' summary", a few per bottle, with the dataset credit.

Gotchas:
- Records keep accords as Fragrantica's bar widths, which our extractor reads and the engine's weights are tuned on: 30 + 0.7 × the dataset's strength (`barWidth` and `barStrength` in `src/accords.js`).
- Note weights are 100 × (votes / the top note's votes)^1.61; records turn them back into relative `noteVotes`.
- Its season and day/night votes are exact and match our scrapes; its ratings can come from an older index.
- Some descriptions hold a raw U+2028, which Node's readline and Python's `splitlines()` treat as a line break; read the raw dump line by line in Python, as `decant.py` does.

## Engine

`src/engine.js` is pure: fragrances + conditions in, ranked tiers with reasons out. A bottle's score is `scoreOf(parts, occasion)`: the weighted parts (`DAILY_WEIGHTS` or `OCCASION_WEIGHTS`) less its rotation penalty; anything that scores bottles outside `rank()` (the gap finder) calls it rather than copying the weights. Tiers are by rank (3 S, 5 A, 6 B, rest C), and a tier also needs its floor score (`TIER_FLOORS`: S 0.75, A 0.6), so in a small collection on a sweltering day a bottle that's "too rich for sticky heat" isn't S just for being third. `test/engine.test.js` pins behaviour on the real collection (hot humid day puts fresh scents on top, cold night puts heavy ones on top, occasion sanity), so run `npm test` after any tuning. Accord heaviness and chip colors live in `src/accords.js`: each accord has a hand-set `prior` and a `weight` that `npm run fit` (`scripts/fit-accords.mjs`) pulls toward the season votes of every bottle in `data/`. Rerun it after adding bottles; `test/accords.test.js` fails until you do, and also when the data has an accord `src/accords.js` lacks. A new accord gets `weight` equal to its `prior`. Occasion profiles live in `src/occasions.js`; every accord over 1.5% of the strength in `data/` needs an entry in each (0 marks it considered and neutral), and `test/occasions.test.js` names any that lack one.

The occasion part reads on one fixed scale for every collection: 0 at the dataset's 2nd percentile of `occasionRaw` for that occasion, 1 at its 98th (`OCCASION_SCALE` in `src/occasions.js`, over the 8,201 bottles with 500+ rating votes). Within `OCCASION_KNEE` of either end it bends toward 0 or 1 instead of stopping, so bottles past the dataset's range keep their order: Parfumo's occasion votes, which dataset bottles lack, carry a few owned bottles past the 98th percentile (a hard cap tied five demo bottles as perfect date-night fits). Anything comparing owned bottles with dataset ones on this scale (the gap finder) should leave out `parfumo.occasion` on the owned side, so both stand on the same evidence. Stretching each list's own min..max made any wardrobe's best bottle a perfect fit, read two bottles as 0 and 1, and let a narrow spread drown out the weather (a summer citrus topped Outdoors at 30°F). After changing a profile, a performance wish or the dataset, refit it with `node scripts/fit-occasions.mjs --write`; `test/occasions.test.js` fails until you do.

Reason text never names a raw accord label or a temperature: `ACCORD_NOUN`, `COOLING` and `WARMING` turn accords into phrases ("Warm spice for the cold", "Bright citrus cuts the heat", "Woods & aromatics suit casual wear"), only fresh accords cut the heat, and the rain reason names the bottle's own strongest rain accord. Reasons about the bottle itself (its accords against the weather or occasion, performance, recent wears) sort before the season and time-of-day votes. `test/reasons.test.js` scans every reason over the wardrobe, the catalog and two dataset shards.

Recent wears push a bottle down for ten days, fading (`ROTATION_PENALTY`, 0.2 today to 0.01 nine days ago; "Worn N days ago"). Top scores sit within a few hundredths, so the old three-day memory cycled three bottles through a month of steady weather; ten days rotates 9 to 17.

The rotation nudge pulls neglected bottles in (`nudge` on `rank` and `planWindows`, on in the CLI whenever there's a journal): a bottle unworn for `NUDGE_AFTER` (30) days or more that scores within `PLAN_MARGIN` of the top and shares the top pick's tier moves to the front ("Not worn in 41 days"), so it never trades an S pick for an A; when the top pick is a neglected bottle already, nothing moves and nothing says so. Days count from the last wear or plan before the window; a bottle never worn counts from the journal's first logged wear, or from `added` when a record carries the date it joined, so nothing is neglected before the journal is a month old, and it says "Not worn yet" (the journal's age isn't the bottle's). Bottles David rates 2★ or lower (`DISLIKED`) sit out on purpose and are never nudged. A plan nudges at most once every three days, and a window with a logged wear keeps it. `test/nudge.test.js` runs it on synthetic histories.

Your own ratings ride on records as `personal` (`src/skin.js`: per bottle on the device, `scentcast.skin`, stars 1–5 and "on my skin" longevity and sillage -1/0/1; `applySkin(records, skin)` attaches them, and the journal export carries them). Stars count twice. `quality()` takes 80% from them (1★ reads 0, 3★ neutral, 5★ 1) and 20% from the crowd's, but quality is only 5% of a score, so `rank` adds `parts.personal` too (`personalPart`, `PERSONAL_PICK`: -0.3 at 1★, -0.12 at 2★, +0.02 at 5★): a 1★ bottle never leads and drops out of S and A, a 2★ one gives way to any other S bottle, a 5★ one wins near ties, and 4★ (what most kept bottles would get) moves a bottle only through quality. `test/skin.test.js` checks the first two over 512 windows. `scoreOf` adds `parts.personal` when it's there, so the gap finder stays on the crowd's footing unless it passes one. Reasons say "You rate it 5★" (4★ and up) or "You rate it 2★" (2★ and down) in place of "Crowd favorite". Each skin nudge moves `performanceLevel` by half a level (`PERSONAL_SHIFT`), which reaches the occasions that care about performance and spray counts, not daily picks; where an occasion cares, the reason is yours ("Lasts longer on you", "Sits closer on you") in place of the crowd's. Scent styles still read voters' sillage.

Sticky heat is judged by dew point (`dewF` on forecast windows, from temperature and humidity), not relative humidity: 88°F at 55% is oppressive, 72°F at 85% is not.

The forecast's temperature, feels-like and humidity are the mean of four global models (`BLEND_MODELS` in `src/weather.js`: GFS, ECMWF, ICON, GEM), averaged in `blendModels` as it's fetched; current conditions, weather codes and rain chances stay Open-Meteo's default. Open-Meteo's default alone (GFS in the US) ran warm and dry against airport readings. `python3 scripts/score-forecasts.py [START END]` rescores the models and blends on 14 US airports (past forecasts from Open-Meteo's Previous Runs API, readings from the Iowa Mesonet ASOS archive); rerun it before changing the blend.

Which season the weather "is" comes from `SEASON_CLIMATE`: the mean and spread of each season's day and night feels-like over three years in ten temperate cities, fitted by `node scripts/fit-seasons.mjs --write` from Open-Meteo's archive (cached in `.cache/climate/`). Spring and fall weather overlap, so the calendar splits that share (`TRANSITION_SPLIT`), fading to an even split as it gets colder (`TRANSITION_COLD`, full at 58°F, even at 38°F): spring votes go to florals and greens, which a cold April night doesn't call for any more than a cold October one. Midwinter and midsummer days lean toward their calendar season by up to `CALENDAR_BOOST`, easing in and out smoothly rather than stepping at month boundaries, and the calendar fades out toward the equator (`TROPICS`, 25° to 15° of latitude). Without a date, `targetSeasonWeights` reads no calendar at all; `comfortRange` and `--ideal` use that, so a bottle's range is the same all year (Amber Empire's swung from 30°F in April to 56°F in October).

Season and day/night shares are read through `steadyShares`: a split from few votes leans toward a prediction, so a 100-vote indie bottle can't swing on a handful of votes. The prediction (`predictedShares` in `src/custom.js`) is the bottle's accords' profiles (`src/profiles.js`: the season and night shares each accord pulls toward, fitted by `scripts/fit-profiles.mjs` on the dataset's 12,000 well-voted bottles), averaged with `like`, the votes of the bottles Fragrantica voters say it smells like (from the dataset, on every record that has them). It counts as `PRIOR_VOTES`, measured on bottles held out of the fit: 36 season and 16 day/night votes from the accords alone, 60 and 26 with `like`. A dupe's original counts as another 120 votes for both: across the 18 dupes it predicts the dupe's split far better than the accords do (refit by comparing them again if the list grows). The bottle page still shows the raw votes. Hand-entered and never-voted bottles carry the prediction as their split.

Occasions can also want a loud or quiet, lasting or fleeting bottle (`performance` in `src/occasions.js`: Office quiet, Night Out loud, Formal and Date lasting). `performanceLevel` reads Parfumo's longevity and sillage as -1..1 around the data's medians (7.8 and 7.4), shrunk toward them when few voted; without Parfumo ratings it reads the dataset's Fragrantica votes (`performance`, put on Parfumo's scale by a line fitted on the 53 bottles both rate), and a bottle with neither sits in the middle. Daily picks don't use them. Parfumo voters' own occasion shares nudge Office, Date Night, Night Out and Outdoors (`VOTED_OCCASIONS`: Business, Evening, Night Out, Sport), where they agree with the accord profiles (r 0.54 to 0.74 on the 53 bottles that have them); Casual, Formal and Cozy have no clean match and use the accords alone.

Season and time fit are half "within its comfort zone" and half "its specialty" (lift over an even vote split). The specialty half keeps flat all-rounders from winning every mild day.

Layering (`src/layering.js`, the "Layer it with" section of a bottle's page) is separate from ranking. Notes map to accord families by name (`NOTE_FAMILIES`, first match wins, so specific names go first; a note between families, like labdanum or ambroxan, lists several, main one first, the rest at half weight), and each bottle's profile blends those with its accords. Notes count more in the heart and base than on top, and by their votes over the bottle's average (`noteVotes`, between `NOTE_VOTE_FLOOR` and `NOTE_VOTE_CAP`); a shared note voters barely smell doesn't count as a bridge. A pair scores on complement (`PAIRS`, symmetric, negatives for clashes), a bridge of shared families or notes (near-duplicates are marked down), and heaviness contrast; the heavier bottle is sprayed first. Each partner's average pairing across the collection is subtracted, so all-rounders don't top every list. Given a weather window (the page always has one), `blendFit` reads the pair like the engine reads one bottle (both bottles' season votes, and their average heaviness against the weather's target) and a pair below `BLEND_OK` loses points; without one, two heavy bottles take a flat penalty instead. `test/layering.test.js` fails when a note in the data maps to no family (add it to `NOTE_FAMILIES`), or when `PAIRS` has no rule for more than a fifth of how the data's families meet (add rules for the gaps it names; a 0 marks a pair as considered and neutral). Rescale `COMPLEMENT_FLOOR` and `COMPLEMENT_SPAN` to the complement's 10th–90th percentile after big table changes.

Parfumo's layering votes feed the same ranking. The build turns each `layer` list into `layeredWith` on wardrobe and catalog bottles (`[{id, consensus, via}]`, partners within those two files, the same both ways), with a dupe standing in for its original on either side: Layton + Althaïr votes pair Détour Noir with Liquid Brun, and `via` names the two bottles voted on. A voted pair gains `COMMUNITY_WEIGHT` × consensus, given a weather window scaled from nothing at a blend fit of `BLEND_FLOOR` to full at `BLEND_OK`, and leads its reasons ("Parfumo voters layer Layton with Althaïr"). A pair off for the weather (fit below `BLEND_OFF`, the pairs that say "Too rich for this heat") sorts after every pair that isn't (`byPick`), votes or not. A note listed in over a third of the collection (bergamot, musk and sandalwood in the demo, `commonNotes`) doesn't count as a shared-note tie. The boost sits on top of the model: `rank` orders picks and can pass 1 to break ties among pairs the model already rates perfect, `score` stays capped at 1, and partner averages leave the boost out. The hand-set rules don't predict the votes (on the wardrobe's 21 voted pairs in October 2026 the model alone ranked them at chance, AUC 0.47), so treat the votes as the better evidence when tuning `PAIRS`.

`planWindows` plans several windows in time order (Today's day + night, the Week tab, `--week`): each pick counts as a `planned` wear for later windows, and a bottle not yet in the plan takes the slot when it scores within `PLAN_MARGIN` (exported, 0.06) of the top. A wear already logged for a window stays its pick. A window can carry its own occasion (`win.occasion`, a trip's formal night) over the plan's.

## Scent styles

`src/scent-styles.js` scores any engine record (wardrobe, catalog, `fromFragrantica`, hand-entered) 0..1 on the words people shop by: 22 scent styles (`kind: 'scent'`: Skin scent, Clean, Fresh citrus, Aquatic, Green, Barbershop, Gourmand, Vanilla, Amber, Oud, Leather, Tobacco, Incense & smoke, White floral, Rose, Floral, Powdery & iris, Woody, Spicy, Fruity, Chypre & mossy, Boozy) plus Intimate (`kind: 'wear'`), read from voters' sillage and ranked apart, so a quiet bottle still shows its character. `STYLES` carries each one's label, tooltip `blurb` and scoring tables.

A style's score is a noisy-OR of its accords' strengths (`acc`; strength^1.3, so an accord has to lead to count fully) plus a noisy-OR of its notes' salience (`notes`: votes over the bottle's most-voted note, the pyramid tier when it has none; a third item is a floor for a defining molecule like Calone), damped by exp(-penalty) for accords that pull away from it (`against`: vanilla isn't Fresh citrus), then eased toward 1 above 0.8. A few styles add structure: fougère (lavender with coumarin or moss), chypre (citrus over moss and patchouli, and a dry bergamot-patchouli "modern chypre" without moss), laundry (fresh air over musk) and soapy iris (iris with neroli, unless amber or vanilla warms it) for Clean, and Amber only as warm as its resins (ambroxan's "amber" in Sauvage isn't). Skin scent and Clean lean on sillage a little. Hand-entered bottles score on accords alone and never wear close.

The rules that turn scores into labels read a scores object, so scores stored ahead of time show and count like live ones: `topOf(scores)` gives the scent styles worth showing (from `STYLE_MIN` 0.55, strong from 0.75, 3 at most), `closeOf` the Intimate flag (from 0.6: the quietest 15% of well-voted bottles) and `leanOf` what a bottle leans toward (its top 2 from 0.4). `topStyles(record)` and `wearsClose(record)` score a record and apply them, and `styleMix(records[, scoresOf])` counts a collection's leans and who wears close. Don't re-derive these rules elsewhere. Accords decide where they disagree with reputation: Daisy is a white floral, Clean Warm Cotton aquatic-citrus. Boozy and Incense lean on notes, so a bottle without a liquor or incense note misses (Khamrah). A record scores in about 10 µs (tables compile to slots, note names' matches are cached): 10,000 dataset bottles take 100–160 ms in Node or desktop Chrome, about 4 times that on a phone; `topOf` and `closeOf` on 10,000 stored scores take about 15 ms.

After changing weights: `npm test` (`test/scent-styles.test.js` wants at least 90% of the canonical bottles in `test/style-exemplars.js` and 80% of the held-out ones to show their style in their top 2, pins the wardrobe and a few boundary bottles, and fails when an accord in `src/accords.js` sits in no style's `acc` or `against`), and `node scripts/check-styles.mjs` for the misses. Run `--save /tmp/before.json` before the change and `--against /tmp/before.json` after: it counts the shown styles gained and lost per style and lists every well-voted bottle whose lean, shown styles or Wears close changed, with old and new scores. Read them all, and eyeball more with `--sample 50` or `--style <id>`. Tune on the exemplars only: a bottle you tune on moves from the held-out set into the exemplars, and an untuned one takes its place.

## Candidates and similarity

The gap finder and Discover pick from the suggestion pool with two pure modules; build on them rather than re-deriving any of it. `src/similar.js` says how alike two bottles smell: `scentVector(record)` is a pair of unit vectors, accords by Fragrantica strength and the 8 strongest notes by votes (top notes count 0.6: the drydown is most of the wear; only 8 so an owned bottle's full pyramid compares with a pool row's), cached per record. `similarity` is 0.6 × the accords' cosine + 0.4 × the notes', accords alone when either has no notes: Liquid Brun and Althaïr 0.92, Club de Nuit Intense Man and Aventus 0.78, Atlantis Extrait and Liquid Brun 0.12. `centroid` blends several bottles into one target, `nearestOf` finds the closest in a list, and `sharedTraits` names what two share for copy ("bourbon vanilla, sweet, warm spicy, praline"; accords come as Fragrantica names). In `sharedTraits` a note folds into the one-word accord its last word names (Bourbon Vanilla into vanilla, White Musk into musky) but never on a kind like Notes or Leaf, and a name that repeats one listed before it is left out ("fresh" beside "fresh spicy").

Similarity misses some voted dupes (Supremacy Collector's Edition and Absolu Aventus 0.31), so `src/candidates.js` reads the "smells like" votes too. Its signals come from `extras`, which only the pool's records and the built demo, catalog and originals carry: a visitor's bottles and share-link ones load from r/ without them. So every function that reads them takes `byId` (the pool, id -> fromPool record; `allowContext(owned, { byId })` passes it on) and reads the bottle's pool row instead, found by id or, for an old `fg:` or hand-entered id, by name and brand (`poolRowOf`); outside the pool, its `about` (loadAbout: value, lean, flags and smells-like links, with decant.py's thresholds; no safety, keep or cheaper twins). Attach `about` to a visitor's bottles outside the pool before asking. `signalsOf(record, byId)` gives them in one shape (string ids; `known: false` when none of the three has anything, as for a hand-entered bottle or one newer than the dataset). `familyIndex(byId, owned)` links bottles by their family links both ways, counting each dupe's `original` as owned, and `dupeOf(record)` places a bottle: `original` of an owned dupe or `near` (a twin of an owned bottle or original), ×0.15 novelty; `flanker` (same brand and line, and similarity 0.3+: Supremacy Silver is one, Supremacy Noir a new direction), ×0.3; `family` (two family links, or a plain `like` link), ×0.6. For David the pool holds 79 near, 18 flankers and 78 family; Club de Nuit Intense Man is family "through Aventus". Only built dupes have an `original`, so from r/ an original counts as a twin where voters link it (17 of David's 19) and the family reaches one hop less. `novelty(record, ownedVectors(owned, byId), family)` is 0.35 + 1.6 × (1 − similarity to the nearest owned bottle or original), at most 1, times that multiplier; `ownedVectors` takes originals from the pool, since a built `original` has no accords or notes. It compares with every owned bottle (300 ms for the whole pool against David's 44), so take it lazily, for candidates that could still win.

`allow(record, allowContext(owned, { byId }))` drops owned bottles, owned dupes' originals, brigaded ones, ratings under 3.8 and leans outside the wardrobe's 10th–90th percentile ± 0.4 (lean runs -1..1; under 3 bottles with a known lean, any). `candidateQuality` multiplies the rating (shrunk by 1,000 votes toward the pool's median), `keep` and value, value by `valueSlope(owned, norms, byId)`: 0.19 for David's 4.2 median against the pool's 3.0, 0.08 for Brooke's; `preferValueDefault` is on from a median of 3.8. Both need 3 bottles with a known value (else 0.08 and off). `cheaperTwin(record, byId, ctx)` takes the first `extras.alt` in the pool that rates 3.8+ and isn't brigaded or discontinued (Aventus: Club de Nuit Intense Man); given an `allowContext`, a twin the wardrobe owns comes first (Althaïr: "You own its twin, Liquid Brun."). `riskOf(record, byId)` scores what makes a blind buy risky (brigaded, not a blind buy, polarizing or a low safety percentile, owners letting it go, discontinued, reformulated, hard to find, longevity in the pool's bottom tenth): high from 3 points (7% of the pool), medium from 2, a single minor reason stays low but is listed, and a bottle with no crowd data is `unknown` ("There’s no crowd data on it yet."), never low. Over the 9,644-bottle pool in Node: vectors 76 ms, `familyIndex` 30 ms, `poolNorms` 13 ms (after `fromPool`'s 200 ms).

## Gap finder and buy check

For "what am I missing", "what should I buy next" or "I need more formal cold-weather scents": `node scripts/recommend.mjs --gaps [--city "<city>"] [--uses "office=often,formal=never"] [--value | --no-value] [--top N] [--json]`. It prints the wardrobe's strengths, a line on how much of what he wears has a strong pick, its gaps (gaps and thin spots first, then room to grow) and the bottles that would fill them, each with the situations it fills ("Office on cold days: 83%, over your Sandalwood & Musk at 78%"; a later pick names the earlier one it beats, "over Infinity For Men (suggested above)"), the owned bottle it's closest to, a cheaper twin and its blind-buy risk. Picks fill only the gaps listed, so a strong wardrobe gets three or four, and "Nothing else would fill these gaps by enough" is an answer, not a failure. Without a place it uses the default climate (the ten temperate cities); with one, that place's last two years (one archive call). How often he dresses for each occasion changes the answer most (Office and Formal settle half his list), so ask when it matters and pass `--uses` (Never, Sometimes or Often against the default shares; Daily picks always count, and "everyday" still means Casual). "Prefer great value" is on for David (his median value is 4.2), which keeps picks to bottles voters value 3.8+; `--no-value` lets in Armani and Guerlain. Reply with the two or three gaps that matter and three picks with why, naming a cheaper twin when there is one. `--exclude` and `--demo` apply as for picks.

For "should I buy X" or "is X worth it": `node scripts/recommend.mjs --check "<name or Fragrantica id>" [--city "<city>"] [--json]` gives a verdict (Strong fit, Good, Redundant or Risky) with two or three reasons, the situations it fills, the closest owned bottle, its risk and twin. It looks in the suggestion pool first, then the whole dataset (signals from the bottle's about data there); a name matching several takes the most voted and says so on stderr, so give the brand or id when it's ambiguous. Relay the verdict and reasons, and add what the numbers can't know (price, whether he likes the notes).

`src/gaps.js` is pure; `src/gaps-worker.js` runs it off the main thread. A cell is a use × day or night × a feels-like band (`BANDS`: cold under 40°F, cool to 58, mild to 72, warm to 84, hot above), 55 in all. `USES` are Daily picks (Wear › Now) and the seven occasions, with default shares of wears (Daily 40%, Office 15%, Date Night 9%, Casual, Outdoors and Cozy 8% each, Night Out 7%, Formal 5%), a day/night split each and band leanings (Outdoors leans warm, Cozy cold). A cell's weight is the use's share × its slot's × how often the band comes up there: `climateSummary` turns a place's day and night windows into each slot × band's share and 6 real windows from it (`PER_CELL`; 4 moved the best bottle in 6 of 55 cells), about 4 KB, and `DEFAULT_CLIMATE` is the ten SEASON_CLIMATE cities' (`node scripts/fit-gaps.mjs --write`, reading `.cache/climate/*-dew-*.json`, which add dew point to fit-seasons' files). `fitTable` scores a bottle in a cell exactly as `rank()` would in those windows (`scoreOf` on the engine's own parts, averaged; `test/gaps.test.js` pins them to 1e-6). Every bottle, owned or not, is scored as `sameFooting` has it, on the pool's evidence: no Parfumo occasion votes, its 8 strongest accords (the dataset's limit; scraped pages show 10, which moved owned bottles' occasion fits by as much as the thresholds below), and flat season or day/night votes zeroed so the engine reads its prediction (`flatVotes`: the least-voted season from half the raters or more within 3 points of the rest, or one identical count on 3 seasons or both slots: Elyon Dubai's 2025 releases and some Louis Vuitton pages, about 20 pool bottles, which otherwise read as perfect all-rounders). The app's `rank()` still reads those votes as they are. The reference is the suggestion pool within the wardrobe's lean range, brigaded bottles out. A cell's shortfall is how far the wardrobe's best, and 0.35 × its second best, sit under the mean of the reference's top 20; it's a gap when 5% of the reference beats the best or the top beat it by 6 points, thin under 2 strong bottles (the reference's 90th percentile) with the next best under the 85th (a runner-up just under the strong line is depth), plenty with 4 strong within 3 points of the top, room to grow when otherwise short by `ROOM_DEFICIT`, and off for a use set to Never. `gapSummary` lists every gap, thin and room cell that comes up, neighbouring bands merged ("Formal on cool and mild nights"), gaps and thin first, then room to grow, each most severe first (weight × shortfall); `overview` says how much of what he wears has a strong pick; `strengths` names each use's deepest plenty run, and both ends' leaders when they differ. Reason and gap text never holds a temperature. The casual occasion is labelled Casual (it was Everyday) so it never clashes with Daily picks.

`suggest` is greedy: each round takes the pool bottle whose fills that matter most cut the shortfall in the listed cells, × `candidateQuality` × `novelty` × its risk (none for high, 0.85 for medium, 0.9 per listed reason) × its lean (less the further it sits outside the wardrobe's own 10th–90th percentile), counts it as owned and goes again: a cell it lifts off the list stops counting, and a later pick's fills are measured against it. Kin of an earlier pick count as twins. Left out: what `allow` drops, owned bottles' twins and originals (`dupeOf`), discontinued and high-risk bottles, anything 0.8+ alike to an owned bottle (`SAME_SIM`; Sauvage EDT and EDP are 0.80), and bottles with no fill that matters. A fill matters (`FILL_MARGIN`) when it lifts the best by 2 points, or adds a strong pick to a thin cell, but not when it's kin of the bottle it backs up (0.75 alike, `KIN_SIM`, or linked by voters: a Cream Velvet look-alike adds no depth to Cream Velvet), and not for Office, Date Night, Night Out or Formal when it fades fast (`weakLongevity`: Book Loft and Snowflakes & Cashmere won Office for being quiet). `buyCheck(record, ctx)` scores a bottle as its pool row when it has one, so a catalog record and its pool row check alike, and decides in order: Redundant when it duplicates something owned (owned, an owned dupe's original or twin, 0.8+ alike, or 0.75+ alike and placed in the family by voters); Risky when `riskOf` says high; Redundant when nothing it adds matters, saying so plainly ("Nothing you own smells like it, but your Sticky Fingers outdoes it even for Formal on cool nights, where it does best"; Dior Homme Intense and Encre Noire land here); Risky when voters rate it under 3.8 (the pool's bottom fifth); Strong fit when it cuts the listed gaps by `STRONG_GAIN` and by as much as the pool's top 2% would for this wardrobe (`STRONG_TOP`; else a third of the pool is a strong fit for a thin wardrobe), with quality at the pool's usual or better and low risk (a bottle without crowd data caps at Good); else Good. Every suggestion checks Strong fit or Good. Over the pool for David: 16 strong fits, 254 good, 720 risky, the rest redundant; for Brooke 147 strong fits.

David in the default climate (October 2026): a strong pick for nearly everything; strong on cold and cool date nights, nights in general and cool-weather outdoors; one real gap, Date Night on mild and warm nights (Infini Rose and Supremacy Collector's Edition), then room to grow on Office on cold and cool days, Cozy on mild nights, Formal on cool nights and Daily picks on hot nights. Value picks: Riiffs Zenith (warm date nights, mild cozy nights), Maison Alhambra Dark Door Intense (cold office days), Armaf Infinity For Men (hot nights); without the value filter, Armani Code Parfum (office and formal), Shalimar Millésime Tonka, Michael Malul Ocean Noir. Houston adds formal in the heat (Assaf Arrogate Pink) and cool office days (Mauboussin Une Histoire d'Homme Irrésistible), Minneapolis puts Dark Door Intense first. Without its 8 fresh bottles the wardrobe's top gaps become warm and hot days and Agua Fresca Citrus Cedro the first pick (tested). Dior Homme Intense loses Office to Dark Door Intense, its clone, because the engine's Office wish rewards weak sillage and voters call Dior a night scent.

In the app: the worker answers `{ type: 'report', owned, summary, shares, preferValue, k }` with `gapReport` (plain data: the coverage grid with each cell's status, the overview line, gaps, strengths, and picks with their records, fills, twins and risk) and `{ type: 'check', record }` with `checkJSON(buyCheck(…))`, attaching about data to a visitor's bottles outside the pool. `summary: null` means the default climate, and `uses` (the "How you wear" settings) can stand in for `shares`; shares that aren't numbers throw. It caches the context on the wardrobe's records as they score, not their ids, so a changed record makes a new one. It loads the pool once and keeps only the records (30–50 MB). The first report takes about 0.75 s from page load in desktop Chromium and 2.7 s at 4× CPU; other shares or the value toggle reuse the pool's scores (about 0.1 s), and a check takes a few ms. `handle(message)` answers one message anywhere: `dev/gaps.html` prints a report through the worker, and `?inline` runs it on the main thread, where DevTools' CPU throttling applies (it doesn't reach workers). `fetchClimate(lat, lon)` in `src/climate.js` makes the one archive call (hourly feels-like and dew point, the last two full years, about 350 KB) and keeps the summary in localStorage per half degree (`scentcast.climate`), falling back to `DEFAULT_CLIMATE`. `src/uses.js` keeps "How you wear" (`scentcast.uses`: Never 0, Sometimes 1, Often 2.5 × each default share, renormalized; Daily always on), and `sharesFrom(settings, counts)` blends journal occasion counts in as (share × 20 + count) / (20 + n) once wears record an occasion. Show status words, titles and a use × band grid, not the severity numbers.

## Discover

`src/discover.js` finds bottles to buy that give more of what someone loves, from the suggestion pool and through the candidate machinery (filters, quality, novelty, family; see above). Pure, so it runs in a module Worker. `discoverContext(pool, owned, { styles })` works out what every query shares once (the pool's records, the wardrobe's filters, family index, owned vectors and lean: about 240 ms in Node or a desktop Chrome Worker, most of it `fromPool`); a query then takes 2–16 ms (the first "more like" also builds the pool's vectors, 70–85 ms, and the first notes search its note index, about 50 ms). Every pick comes as { record, score, styles (shown chips), close, nearest (the owned bottle it's closest to, `through` an owned dupe's original, and what they share), family (a smells-like link to the wardrobe), clonesOf (what a clone copies, going by the votes: `originalFromVotes`; `addCloneVotes(pick, about)` adds the votes), value, rating, risk, twin (a cheaper twin), alike (twins of it that also qualified, most voted first, folded in so a clone and its original take one slot) }, and `whyLine(pick)` says it in one line ("voters say it smells like Ultra Male (2851 to 175); closest you own: Hawas Elixir (through its original, Le Male Elixir: vanilla, sweet, tonka bean)"). Nothing owned, no owned dupe's original, no twin or version of an owned bottle and nothing over 0.9 alike to one is suggested (notes search shows them only with `excludeOwned: false`, labelled), and a bottle voters link to an owned one counts 0.6 of its score. Brigaded and discontinued bottles stay out, and ratings under 3.8 or a style's own floor (below), unless the filters say otherwise.

Style and notes lists lean the way the wardrobe does: the best feminine, unisex and masculine picks (crowd lean under -0.25, between, over 0.25) take turns toward the wardrobe's share of each (`mergeTurns`; David's 17%, 36% and 47%), so a mostly masculine wardrobe asking for skin scents gets Narciso Rodriguez For Him Intense, Dior Homme 2020 and Bleu Noir beside Xtra Milk and Clean Skin rather than a list of women's musks (before: 10 of the top 12 feminine). Only picks scoring 0.6 of their style's best take turns, and beside Intimate only ones that wear close, so a thin band doesn't bring in bottles that barely match. A lean filter replaces the turns (pass `--lean` when David asks for a gender); "more like" follows its seeds instead.

- **By style** (`moreOfStyles(ids, ctx, { mode, k, filters })`, `--style "skin,clean,intimate" [--all]`): any (a bottle shows at least one chosen style; the styles take turns so one can't fill the list; Intimate beside scent styles shapes them, a bottle that doesn't wear close keeping 0.6 of its match, so "skin, clean, intimate" gives quiet skin and clean scents rather than every quiet lemon cologne) or all (each at least 0.4, blended by a soft minimum: Fleur de Peau, Musc Outreblanc, Clean Skin, L'Eau d'Hiver), × √quality × novelty. Quality and the rating floor are judged against the style's own median rating: voters rate skin, clean and intimate bottles lower (median 3.96 against the pool's 4.02; a quarter sit under 3.8), so their floor is about 3.74 and Missing Person and Not A Perfume can show. `--min-rating` (`filters.minRating`) sets one. Typed styles forgive case and plurals ("skin scents"), and musky, soapy and fougère name Skin scent, Clean and Barbershop. Pool scores come from `data/fragrantica/pool-styles.json` (`node scripts/fit-pool-styles.mjs --write`: each pool bottle scored on its full r/ row, styles from 0.3 as 0–100, 227 KB raw, 70 KB gzip). `loadPoolStyles()` turns down a file made for another pool or one it can't fetch, and `discoverContext` one whose `check` (`styleCheck`: a hash of how `src/scent-styles.js` scores every 50th pool row) differs, as a service worker's old copy would; the queries then score live. Rerun it after changing `src/scent-styles.js` or remaking the pool; `test/discover.test.js` re-scores every pool row and fails until you do. The chips start on `likedStyles(owned, { stated })`: the styles the most bottles lean toward, or, given `stated`, those of the stated styles the wardrobe holds a bottle of. David's mix leans vanilla, citrus and spicy, and the styles he says he loves are his thinnest (Skin scent: Sandalwood & Musk, Meant To Be Seen; Clean: Cedrus Blanc; Intimate: Jake, Shiyaaka Sky), so the demo and the CLI pass `DEMO_LIKED` (`--style liked`, which falls back to the mix when none is left). Someone's own choice, once saved, wins.
- **By example** (`moreLike(seeds, ctx, { k, filters })`, `--more-like "Liquid Brun,Amber Empire"`): nearest to the seeds' centroid (owned or any dataset bottle; a seed without accords, like a dupe's built `original`, stands in as its pool row) × √quality, and a bottle voters link to an owned one counts 0.6, so "more like Sauvage" gives Dylan Blue and Terre d'Hermès rather than Salvo Intense. "Fans also like" lifts a pick up to 15% (`ALSO_LIKED`) by the share of seeds whose about data lists it in `alsoLiked`; give dataset seeds their `about` (`loadAbout`) for that. It's a small prior with no clear measured effect: holding each of David's 20 pool bottles out and seeding with its three nearest owned bottles moved mean reciprocal rank from 0.109 to 0.111, within noise; at 40% the best placed start to slip (Détour Noir from 1st to 3rd). `defaultSeeds(owned, { history, todayISO })` gives the three most worn of the last 60 days, else the best rated (`--more-like favorites`, with `--journal` for the wears).
- **By notes and accords** (`searchNotes(query, ctx, filters, { k })`, `--notes "iris leather"`): the longest runs of words that name an accord, a note or an alias ("tonka bean", "lily of the valley", "white floral") are terms, every one must match, and a bottle scores the geometric mean of each term's strength (its accord's strength, or its notes' share of the votes) × √quality. Case, accents, plurals and adjective endings don't matter ("Irises"; smoke, smoky and smokey). `SYNONYMS` finds a note under any of its names (oud, agarwood and oudh; iris and orris; tobacco and tabac; leather, suede and cuir; sandalwood and sandal wood), and `BROADER` lets "fresh" and "warm" take any accord of the kind, so "marine fresh" is a fresh marine. A word that names nothing reads as the name one edit away (two from 7 letters; "lavendar", "tobaco", "sandlewood"), reported in `corrected`; one that names only a note on under 100 bottles stays as typed, with a far commoner near name in `suggested` ("vanila" is a misspelt note on 43 bottles; clover isn't clove). A word near nothing returns no results and comes back in `unknown`.
- **Filters** (`FILTERS`, all three queries; the CLI's `--season`, `--slot`, `--lean`, `--min-value`, `--min-rating`): seasons (near the bottle's best season), day or night (night from 45% of its day/night votes), gender lean (`LEAN_WINDOWS`: feminine, unisex or masculine, overlapping; without one, the wardrobe's window and the lean turns), longevity and sillage minimums on Parfumo's 0–10, value, safety, rating, discontinued, and owned (search only: style and example picks never include owned bottles or their twins).
- **The dupe explorer** (`dupesOf(record, ctx, { more })`, `--dupes "Aventus"`): { original, cheaper, siblings }. The original is an owned dupe's own (`built`), else `originalFromVotes`: the first bottle it smells like (family links, then plain ones) from another brand, released no later, valued 0.5 lower and, through a plain link, 0.3 alike; never for a bottle with no year. Cheaper is one list, most agreed and alike first (net votes × similarity): voters' cheaper twins (`alt`), for a bottle that isn't a dupe its clones (its `clones`, and pool bottles whose family links name it, from other brands, released no earlier, valued 1 higher and 0.3 alike), and up to 5 pool bottles 0.75 alike by notes and valued 1 higher that no vote links (`why: 'notes'`: Khamrah for Angels' Share); a voted one under 0.3 alike is marked `notesDiffer` (Cloud for Baccarat Rouge 540). Siblings are everything else voters link it or its original with, from 5 net votes and 2:1, most agreed first. Each entry has value, rating, risk, similarity and the smells-like votes its about data has. `dupeIds` lists what it looks at outside the pool; load those from r/ into `more`. Voters' links aren't all clones (Lalique White in Black for Layton), so say "voters say it smells like", not "clone of", outside the cheaper list.

`src/wishlist.js` keeps the want list in localStorage (`scentcast.want`): { id, added, note?, record? }, newest first, a full record only for a bottle the dataset can't load again by id (as "mine" keeps its records). `addWant`, `removeWant`, `hasWant`, `setWant` (toggle and save), `wantIdsToLoad`.

To answer from the terminal: "more skin scents like Sandalwood & Musk" is `--style skin` for the style, or `--more-like "Sandalwood & Musk"` for the bottle; "what smells like Y but cheaper" is `--dupes Y` (the cheaper list; `--min-value 3.8` with `--more-like Y` for wider nets); "something with iris and leather" is `--notes "iris leather"`, adding `--season`, `--slot` or `--lean` as asked. Lead with the value picks (David buys clones) and say what a clone copies (the `smellsLike` field), name the bottle he owns that each is closest to, flag a medium or high risk, and credit the dataset. Pass `--lean masculine` (or feminine) when he asks for a gender, and `--min-rating` when he wants only crowd favourites or will take lower-rated classics. The pool doesn't say how easy a bottle is to buy: Brazilian houses (Phebo, Granado) and body mists (Bath & Body Works) can lead Clean and Intimate, so mention it when he wants something he can find in a US store.

## Checking picks against real wears

The wear journal keeps a year of wears, each with roughly where it happened. Its Export button downloads them, with the device's place and hidden bottles. `node scripts/journal-check.mjs <export.json>` looks up each wear's weather in Open-Meteo's archive, ranks the demo as the app would have (string ids, like the app, so earlier wears count as rotation; `rankWears`, tested), and reports where the worn bottle landed (average rank against random, top-pick and S/A rates). Use it before and after tuning the engine.

A wear records the use it was logged for (`toggleWear(id, date, slot, where, occasion)`: the occasion's id from the Occasion tab, `'daily'` from Today and the Week), and `occasionCounts(history[, since])` counts wears per use for the gap finder's use shares (`{ daily, office, date, …, untagged }`). Wears logged before uses were recorded count as `untagged`, not daily, so a year of them doesn't swamp the shares; the gap finder reads only use ids. `journalExport(history, loc, hidden, { nowISO, skin })` adds David's ratings (`loadSkin()`) when there are any. Exports from before either change read the same way (`parseJournal`).

## Checking the UI

`npm run dev` serves http://127.0.0.1:5173. `?loc=lat,lon,Name` pins a location and skips the location prompt, which makes headless screenshots possible, and `?theme=gruvbox` opens the other theme. Every view has a hash route: `#/wear/now` (home), `#/wear/week`, `#/wear/occasion[/<id>]`, `#/wear/trip`, `#/wardrobe/owned|wishlist|worn|insights`, `#/discover[?q=…]`, `#/bottle/<id>[?ctx=<list>]` and `#/settings`; the old `?view=today|week|occasion` still opens the matching Wear pane, and `?c=…&n=…` opens a shared list with its banner. `dev/scenes.html` renders every weather and time-of-day scene for tuning `src/scene.js`. `dev/og.html` is the link-preview card; re-render `icons/og.png` from it with the command in its comment. The service worker is network-first for app files, so a reload shows edits, and keeps one copy of the app page for every `?…` visit (other files opened in a tab skip it, so they can't stand in for the app offline); bump `CACHE` in `sw.js` when adding app files (`test/shell.test.js` fails until every file under `src/` is in `SHELL`, `index.html` preloads every module `main.js` imports, and links every stylesheet).

`node dev/shoot.mjs` drives the system Chromium headless and takes screenshots (options in its header): `node dev/shoot.mjs --url 'http://127.0.0.1:5173/?loc=40.71,-74.01,New%20York#/wear/now' --size 390x844 --size 1440x900 --wait .pick --click .hero-pick --wait 800 --out shots/bottle.png`. Each size gets a fresh browser context; `--click` sends a real tap or click, `--key`, `--type` and `--eval` drive the page, `--init` runs before the page's scripts (seed localStorage, stub `fetch`), and console errors or failed requests make it exit 1. Look at the shots with the Read tool. The fixed tab bar sits over the bottom of a phone screen, so click what's under it with `--eval "document.querySelector(…).click()"`.

Layout: three destinations, Wear (Now · Week · Occasion · Trip), Wardrobe (Owned · Wishlist · Worn · Insights) and Discover, plus Settings behind a gear and a bottle page for any bottle. Under 1100px the nav is a floating glass tab bar; from 1100px it's a left rail (with the wordmark, the location and Settings) and the bottle page docks beside the page as a 420px inspector instead of opening over it (a bottom sheet on phones, a right drawer on tablets, both draggable or closed by Back). Now's hero stands the top pick on the hill, rim-lit from the sun or moon (`stagePoint`, `skyLight` in `src/scene.js`; the moon sits across the sky from it, a sun that would hang behind it waits beside it, and no trees stand behind the temperature), with a Day/Night toggle; on a wide column today's other window sits beside the pick; the rest of a window's ranking stands on the magnitude shelf (one moonlight hue at four brightnesses for S–C, B and C dimmed). The page's accent is the sky's own color (`--ambient`, from `skyAccent`); gold (`--accent`) is kept for today, selection and focus.

Code: `src/main.js` only boots `src/app/shell.js`, which owns the layout, routing (`src/app/router.js`, pure and tested), the render cycle and event delegation. State is `src/app/state.js` (no DOM). Each destination is a module in `src/views/` (`wear.js`, `wardrobe.js`, `discover.js`, `bottle.js`, `settings.js`) exporting its page or panes, pure templates, and maps of `actions` (`data-action="name"`), `forms` (`data-form`), `fields` (`data-field`) and `sheets`; a name used twice throws, and `test/router.test.js` fails on a `data-action` nothing handles. A pane is an entry in its destination's `panes` (`{ id, label, title, heading, render }`), so adding one needs no shell change. The bottle page is built from `SECTIONS` in `src/views/bottle.js`: a section module calls `addSection({ id, order, render(model) })`, plus the `actions`, `forms` or `fields` its markup names (the shell merges them with the views'), and gets one import line in `src/views/sections.js`. Shared templates (buttons, rows, chips, the shelf, bars, sheet frames, icons) are `src/ui/components.js`; formatting (escaping, units, dates, `matchPct`, `displayReasons`, which the Omarchy widget imports) is `src/ui.js`. A list of bottles stores its ranking as a context (`storeContext`, keys `day`, `night`, `occ`, `w:<date>:<slot>`) and links to `#/bottle/<id>?ctx=<key>` through `data-action="open" data-ctx data-id`. Each route sets the tab title and the page's h1, moves focus to it and restores that page's scroll.

Styles: `src/tokens.css` (linked first) holds the type scale (`--fs-*`, seven sizes plus the hero's; nothing under 12px), 4pt spacing (`--s-0`…`--s-10`), radii (`--r-pill`, `--r-surface`, `--r-inset`, `--r-control`, `--r-thumb`, `--r-chip`, `--r-round`), elevation (`--e1`…`--e3`), motion (`--dur-1`…`--dur-4`), layout (`--col`, `--rail`, `--inspector`, `--tabbar`) and color roles (`--surface-0`…`3`, `--text-primary/secondary/tertiary`, `--accent`, `--ambient`, `--positive`, `--negative`, `--info`, tiers `--mag-1`…`4`) for both themes. Then `src/css/base.css`, `components.css` (the kit), `shell.css` (grid, nav, page head, the bottle page's frame, settings) and one file per view (`wear.css`, `wardrobe.css`, `discover.css`, `bottle.css`). They use only tokens: lines and fills are the text color at an alpha (`--border-*`, `--fill-*`), so a theme overrides the roles and everything follows, and `test/shell.test.js` fails on a raw color or an off-scale font size. Views lay out by their column with container queries (`@container page`, `@container bottle`), so the inspector narrowing the page needs no extra breakpoints. Buttons are one family: `.btn.primary`, `.btn.secondary`, `.btn.tertiary` (`.compact` draws 36px; `.block` is full width) and `.icon-btn`; every control's hit area is at least 44px (the `::before` inset rule near the top of components.css), and a sheet's footer actions go in a sticky `.sheet-bar`. Text passes WCAG AA in both themes, including over a bright midday sky (the hero's shade and glass tint are set for that), so states fade photos, never text ("Updating…" fades the page's photos and sweeps a line above it). Bottle names are Fraunces italic.

Fonts are self-hosted: `fonts/` has Fraunces (roman and italic, opsz and wght) and Inter (opsz, wght) as latin variable woff2 from Google Fonts' CSS API, with their OFL licenses; `src/tokens.css` declares them and `index.html` preloads them. To refresh one, request the CSS with a current Chrome user agent and take the `/* latin */` file.

Overlay sheets (location, add bottles, paste a list, add it yourself) are bound to history: opening one pushes an entry, so Back (browser, Android, home-screen app) closes it, or steps from "Add it yourself" back to the sheet it came from. A view registers a sheet kind in its `sheets` export (unknown kinds throw). Closing goes Back, which is async, so a navigation after it goes in `closeSheet({ then })`.

Temperatures are °F throughout the engine and weather code; only `src/ui.js` converts for display (`setUnits`). Engine reason text must not include a temperature number for that reason.

## Omarchy widget

The repo root doubles as an Omarchy shell plugin (`doeszen.scentcast`): `manifest.json` points at `omarchy/BarWidget.qml`, which loads `omarchy/Panel.qml`. The panel runs `node omarchy/widget.mjs`, which resolves a place (widget setting, else `~/.local/state/omarchy/settings/weather.json`, else wttr.in's IP lookup), fetches the forecast and prints the week plan as JSON (`widgetPayload`, tested in `test/widget.test.js`). Without `--hidden` it drops `DEFAULT_HIDDEN`, like a new device.

The installed copy in `~/.config/omarchy/plugins/doeszen.scentcast/` is separate from this checkout (the plugin validator refuses symlinks). To try edits, copy the files there (`git ls-files -co --exclude-standard`, which keeps `worker/.dev.vars` out); the shell hot-reloads it. `omarchy-shell doeszen.scentcast open|close|refresh` drives the popup, `omarchy plugin validate .` checks the manifest, and QML errors land in `/run/user/$UID/quickshell/by-pid/<shell pid>/log.log`. Built-in widgets to crib from live in `/usr/share/omarchy/shell/plugins/` (read only).
